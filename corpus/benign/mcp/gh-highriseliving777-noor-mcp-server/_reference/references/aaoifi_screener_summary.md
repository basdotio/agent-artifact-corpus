# AAOIFI Key Definitions — Screener Summary
**Source:** AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards (2015). Internal reference. Not for external distribution.

---

## Riba (Usury/Interest) — Prohibited

Riba is any guaranteed increase on a loan, or any unequal exchange of the same commodity. Two types:

**Riba al-Nasi'ah** — interest charged for deferral of payment. Any amount charged above the principal when a debt is deferred.
**Riba al-Fadl** — unequal exchange of the same commodity (e.g., trading 1kg of gold for 1.1kg of gold). Must be equal, hand-to-hand, same type.

**Apply:** Any task involving interest-bearing loans, mortgages with interest, credit card revolving balances, bond yields, or unequal exchange of currency/metals is Riba-adjacent.

**Citation:** SS 16 (Commercial Papers) 6/2; SS 1 (Trading in Currencies) — currency must be exchanged hand-to-hand in equal amounts.

---

## Gharar (Excessive Uncertainty) — Prohibited When Excessive

**Definition:** Gharar is a state of uncertainty that exists when the process of concluding a transaction involves an unknown aspect. It refers to a status of results that may or may not materialize.

**Three degrees:**
- **Minor Gharar** — unavoidable, no dispute potential. Does NOT void contract (e.g., unseen foundation of a house).
- **Medium Gharar** — between minor and excessive. Does NOT void contract (e.g., sale of underground commodities, Ju'alah contracts).
- **Excessive Gharar** — dominant aspect of the contract, capable of leading to dispute. VOIDS the contract.

**Four conditions for excessive Gharar to void a contract:**
1. Involved in an exchange-based contract (sale, lease, partnership) — not donation contracts.
2. Excessive in degree.
3. Relates to the primary subject matter of the contract.
4. Not justified by a Shari'ah-recognizable necessity.

**Examples of excessive Gharar:**
- Selling fruits before production
- Signing a lease for an unspecified period
- Sale of Salam commodity not usually available at delivery
- Combining two sales in one sale
- Random selection of the sold item (Bay' al-Hasah)
- Selling what you do not own (unless Salam/Istisna'a)

**Citations:** SS 31 (Controls on Gharar) 2/1, 2/2, 4, 5.

---

## Qimar (Gambling) / Murahanah (Betting) — Prohibited

**Definition:** Qimar and Murahanah involve a result that could be accomplished or not, where each party uses it as a means to acquire money from the other. Gharar is similar but broader — not all Gharar is Qimar.

**Apply:** Any task involving casinos, sports betting, lotteries, poker, fantasy sports, or any wager where one party's gain is the other's loss.

**Citation:** SS 31 (Controls on Gharar) — definition appendix.

---

## Jahalah (Ignorance) vs. Gharar

**Jahalah** — lack of knowledge about the details of something, despite knowledge of its occurrence.
**Gharar** — more comprehensive; includes all things that are unknown.
**Relationship:** All that is unknown involves Gharar. Not all Gharar is unknown.

---

## Ghurur / Taghrir (Deception) — Prohibited

**Definition:** Deception results from a statement, act, or position used to deceive others. Unlike Gharar, it involves deliberate deception.

**Apply:** Any task involving fraudulent representations, misleading marketing, or false advertising.
