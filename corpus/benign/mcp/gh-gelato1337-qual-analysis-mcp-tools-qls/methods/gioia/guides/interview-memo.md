# The interview memo

One memo per interview (`memo(about=[source], kind="summary", level="interview")`), written at the
end of the session while the whole interview is still in front of you. It is the only trace of this
interview's soft knowledge that later sessions will have: the person, the tone, what mattered to
them, what did not fit. Concepts carry the claims; the memo carries everything around them.

Write prose, not a form. Cover what is there, in roughly this order, and skip what is not:

1. **Who.** Role, background, vantage point, as far as the interview shows it. What they can speak
   to with authority, and what they are speculating about.
2. **What they care about.** The two or three things they kept returning to, and their stance
   (enthusiastic, wary, resigned, matter-of-fact).
3. **The arc.** How the interview went: where they opened up, where they deflected, where the
   interviewer's framing steered the answers.
4. **What surprised you.** Anything that contradicts what you expected or what other informants said
   (cite the concept ids).
5. **What does not fit the codebook.** The residual, in a sentence or two. Also write it as its own
   `residual` memo about the source, so residuals can be pooled across interviews.
6. **What you are unsure about.** Passages you may have misread, ambiguous Finnish, irony. Also as an
   `uncertainty` memo if it matters for the analysis.

Keep it under the length budget (`interview` level). Cite concept ids where you refer to them, so a
reader can go from the memo to the evidence.

Write separate short memos as you go, too: an `informant` memo the moment you understand who this
person is, a `meaning` memo when you decide what a code means here. The interview memo can then cite
them instead of repeating them.
