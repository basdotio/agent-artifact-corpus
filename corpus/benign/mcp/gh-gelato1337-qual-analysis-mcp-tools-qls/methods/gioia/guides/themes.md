# Building 2nd-order themes

Now you move from the informants' terms to yours. The question is Gioia's: **what is going on here,
theoretically?** A theme names a phenomenon that several concepts point to, often from different
sides, and that bears on the research question.

## Work from the concepts, not the transcripts

You will not have the interviews in context. You have the concepts, their descriptions and quotes,
the codebook and the memos. Start with an overview (`query`, `codebook()`, the batch memos), then pull
evidence as you need it (`pack(id)` for a concept's quotes and memos). Go back to a transcript
(`read_source`) when a concept's meaning is unclear: the description is a summary, the quote is the
evidence.

## What makes a theme

- **A phenomenon, not a topic.** "Views on the triage protocol" is a topic. "Experienced nurses
  quietly replacing the protocol with their own judgment" is a phenomenon. Test: does the label make a claim someone
  could disagree with?
- **Concepts from several informants.** A theme carried by one informant is possible but needs a memo
  saying why it matters anyway.
- **Different sides of one thing.** Concepts that agree, concepts that show the same process from
  another actor's view, concepts that show its limits or the resistance to it. Contradiction inside a
  theme is fine when the theme is about the tension.
- **A reason per concept.** One line on why this concept belongs here. When the reason sounds like
  "also about X", the theme is a topic.

Theory from `intent()` may help you see what a group of concepts is about. Use it, but the theme must
still stand on the concepts: a reader should be able to go from the theme to the quotes and agree.

## Concepts that fit nowhere

Do not force them in. Leave them out of themes and write a `meaning` memo on each saying why it does
not fit. Several orphans pointing the same way may be a theme you have not seen yet.

## Revising concepts

Grouping often shows that a concept was two ideas, or that two concepts are one. You may revise,
split or merge concepts in this stage, each with a reason. Do not rewrite a concept to make it fit a
theme.

## Before you checkpoint

- Every concept is in a theme or has a memo saying why not (`check()`).
- Write a `batch` memo on the theme structure as a whole: what you see, which themes you are least
  sure of, which alternative grouping you considered and rejected.
