Stage: calibrate. Interviews: {sources}.

This is the calibration round. Its purpose is to show the researcher that you have understood the
assignment before the rest of the interviews are coded: that your concepts are at the right level,
that they keep the informants' meaning, and that they bear on the research question. Code these
interviews exactly as you would code any other; a calibration that is easier than the real work
calibrates nothing.

1. Orient: `intent()`, `method_guide()`, `ways_of_working()`, `status()`, `codebook()` (it is
   empty), `list_sources()`. Read `guide("concept")` and `guide("interview-memo")`.
2. For each interview, in the order given: `begin(source)`, read it all, add its 1st-order concepts
   with quotes and reasons, write its memos (informant, residual, interview memo). Before moving to
   the next interview, check whether earlier concepts recur, and use `add_evidence` when they do.
3. After the last interview, read `guide("codebook")` and propose codebook entries for the concepts
   that recur across these interviews, or that you expect to recur.
4. Propose the fields a concept should carry in this study. Every concept already has a label,
   description, in-vivo term and quotes with reasons. If the research question needs more on every
   concept (for example, a stance or a time horizon the question turns on), write a `method` memo
   about the intent listing each proposed field: its name, what it records, its allowed values, and
   why the question needs it. Propose none if none is needed; say why. Once approved, the fields are
   fixed for the rest of the run.
5. Write a `batch` memo citing the interview memos: how you read the interviews against the question,
   the level of detail you chose for concepts and why, and what you are least sure of.
6. `checkpoint(summary, questions)`. Ask what you need the researcher to decide: concepts you are
   unsure are at the right level, codebook entries that could go either way, how to treat recurring
   kinds of passages. Then stop.

If you are resuming after the checkpoint: read `feedback()` first, apply the researcher's decisions
(revise, merge or withdraw concepts with a reason that cites the feedback), and stop again if asked.
