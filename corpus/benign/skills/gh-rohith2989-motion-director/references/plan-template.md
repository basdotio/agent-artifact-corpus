# <Film title> — treatment

> Written before any scene is built. Every decision in the film traces back to a line in here.

## 1 · Brief (inferred, never asked)
- **Subject:** what it is, in one plain sentence (from its own README / site / copy — quote it).
- **Audience:** who watches, where (LinkedIn feed, in-app onboarding, a keynote screen), with or without sound.
- **Job of the film:** the one thing a viewer must know or do after watching.
- **Type:** launch film · onboarding/tutorial · feature teaser · explainer · logo sting · social cut · title sequence.
- **Format:** 16:9 1920×1080 @ 60 (or 9:16 / 1:1 / 4:5) · duration · narrated? · captions burned in?

- References studied (premium films in this category) and the 3–5 techniques taken from each: …

## 4 · Shot list (per section)
For every section: shots with camera (from → to, zoom, ease), the UI/type that moves, the transition in and
out (panel slide-over, circle wipe from an element, zoom-through, match cut, card morph, split-flap…), the
sound (music state, the cues that make sound), and the 2–3 frames that must look perfect paused.

## 5 · Motion language
Easing families (entrances expoOut, UI quintOut, camera inOutSoft/heavyInOut), durations, stagger, camera
style (lean-ins on clicks, parallax amount), what NEVER moves, the one hero motion per beat.

## 6 · Sound
Key, BPM, chord loop, section dynamics (full / filtered / breakdown / drop / final chord), risers and impacts
on which downbeats, the UI sounds per event, voice (if any) and the voice's delivery note.

## 7 · Voice (narrated films)
The script, one line per beat, ≤ 2.6 words/s; which word each on-screen event lands on.

## 8 · Risks and checks
What could read as generic, cheap or wrong — and how each will be verified (stills, strips, AI review).
