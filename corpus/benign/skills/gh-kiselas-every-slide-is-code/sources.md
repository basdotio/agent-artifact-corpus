# Sources and further reading

## Story and argument

- Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*: the answer first, SCQA, grouping the supporting arguments.
- Nancy Duarte, *Resonate*: the "what is / what could be" structure of persuasive talks.
- Nancy Duarte, *slide:ology*: slide design for projected talks.
- Garr Reynolds, *Presentation Zen*: restraint, one idea per slide, the speaker as the medium.
- Chip and Dan Heath, *Made to Stick*: concrete, unexpected, credible messages.

## Charts

- Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*: data-ink, honest scales, small multiples.
- Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data*: choosing the chart from the message, decluttering, highlighting the one series that matters.
- Dona M. Wong, *The Wall Street Journal Guide to Information Graphics*: rules for bars, lines and pies in business charts.
- F. N. Fritsch, R. E. Carlson, "Monotone Piecewise Cubic Interpolation" (1980): the curve behind `linePath`.

## Motion and transitions

- Frank Thomas, Ollie Johnston, *The Illusion of Life*: the principles of animation that also apply to builds.
- Apple Keynote's Magic Move: the model for `morph`.
- impress.js and Prezi: slides placed in a continuous world, the idea behind `camera`.
- reveal.js: the reference for keyboard conventions, fragments and speaker view in HTML decks.

## This kit's sibling

- [motion-kit / every-frame-is-code](https://github.com/kiselas/every-frame-is-code): the same approach for videos and motion graphics. `draw(t)` became `render(slide, step, t)`.
- [Agent Skills](https://agentskills.io): the open skill format used here.
