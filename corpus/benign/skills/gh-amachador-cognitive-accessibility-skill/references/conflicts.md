# When heuristics conflict

The nine heuristics sometimes pull in opposite directions. That is expected — they
protect different cognitive demands, and neurodivergent profiles differ enough that one
person's relief is another's load.

Use this file to write the report's **Tensions** section: name the conflict, decide
which heuristic wins **for this user and task**, and say why.

---

## How to decide

1. **Name both heuristics and the demand each protects.** "H3 protects decision-making
   by showing every step; H4 protects focus by showing less at once."
2. **Check the task.** Which demand is the one that decides success here? A one-off,
   high-stakes form and a daily dashboard resolve the same conflict differently.
3. **Apply the tie-breakers below, in order.**
4. **Prefer a resolution that satisfies both** — most conflicts have one. Only pick a
   winner when none exists.
5. **Write it down** in the report's Tensions section: the conflict, the winner, the
   reason, and what the losing heuristic still gets.

**Tie-breakers, in order:**

| | Protect first | Because |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Against **irreversible harm** — lost work, money, legal consequence, notifying others (H8) | It can't be fixed afterwards |
| 2 | **Task completion** — the person can finish at all | A barrier that stops the task outranks one that slows it |
| 3 | Against **overload** — sensory or cognitive load severe enough to end the session (H1, H4) | Overload ends the task too, just later |
| 4 | Against **friction** — effort, delay, repetition | Real, but recoverable |

**When profiles diverge, give control.** If one person needs less and another needs
more, the answer is rarely a better fixed default — it's a choice the person can make,
with a sensible default (H5).

## The common conflicts

### H3 Assistance ↔ H4 Progressive discovery

*Show every step and its outcome* vs. *show a little at a time*.

| Lean H3 — show the whole path | Lean H4 — reveal gradually |
|---|---|
| High stakes or irreversible outcome | Low stakes, easily undone |
| First use; the person needs to predict what's coming | Daily use; the path is already known |
| Short flow (≤ 5 steps) | Long flow or large amount of information |
| Anxiety under uncertainty is the main risk | Overload is the main risk |

**Satisfies both:** show the **map** in full and the **content** one step at a time —
a step indicator listing every step by name, with only the current step's fields open.

**Example:** EXPE+ — the number format should be fully visible at the field (H3), while
the help on *searching by word* can stay behind "Ayuda" (H4).

---

### H5 Control and customization ↔ H7 Consistency and standards

*Let people reshape the interface* vs. *keep it where they expect it*.

| Lean H5 — allow customization | Lean H7 — hold the standard |
|---|---|
| Daily use; people build their own routines | Occasional use; relearning costs more than it saves |
| Profiles diverge (sensory needs, views, density) | Everyone benefits from the same answer |
| The change is personal and doesn't affect others | Others share the screen or give instructions about it |

**Satisfies both:** start from the standard, let people change it, and keep a
**"restore default"** always visible.

**Example:** Teams relocating call controls broke H7 *and* offered no H5 — people got
neither the standard nor the choice. Either alone would have been acceptable.

---

### H8 Resilience (confirm) ↔ H1 Purposeful stimulus (don't interrupt)

*Ask before consequential actions* vs. *every dialog is an interruption*.

| Lean H8 — confirm | Lean H1 — don't interrupt |
|---|---|
| Irreversible (delete, pay, submit to an authority) | Reversible |
| Notifies or affects other people | Affects only the person |
| Rare action | Frequent action — confirmations get dismissed unread |

**Satisfies both:** **undo instead of confirm** wherever the action can be reversed —
soft delete, an undo toast, a send delay. Keep confirmation for what can't be undone or
reaches other people, and make it specific: name what will happen, not *"Are you sure?"*

**Example:** Gmail's send delay (reversible, no interruption) alongside Slack's
confirmation before a mass mention (reaches others, so it asks).

---

### H1 Reduce stimulus ↔ H9 Use stimulus to hold attention

*Minimize what competes for attention* vs. *use stimulus deliberately to keep attention
on what needs action*. Both come from the same research; they're two halves of one idea.

| Lean H1 — reduce | Lean H9 — signal |
|---|---|
| Decoration, promotion, ambient motion | The one thing that needs action now |
| Many elements competing | A single urgent state (error, deadline, required field) |
| Sustained reading or writing | A moment the person must not miss |

**Satisfies both:** a **stimulus budget** — at most one salient element per screen, and
it's the one requiring action. Everything else is calm. Let the person turn signals
down.

**Example:** Movie's hero carousel spends the whole budget on promotion, so nothing is
left for the purchase panel's missing step (COMPRAR looks ready when it isn't).

---

### H2 Clarity (state everything) ↔ H4 Progressive discovery (say less)

*State expectations, outcomes and consequences explicitly* vs. *don't present everything
at once*.

| Lean H2 — say it now | Lean H4 — defer it |
|---|---|
| Needed for the **next** decision | Needed later, or only in edge cases |
| Consequences of acting (cost, time, reversibility) | Background, history, legal basis |
| A rule the input will be checked against | Explanation of why the rule exists |

**Satisfies both:** be explicit about **what the next decision depends on**; put the
rest one step away and label it plainly.

**Example:** Aduanas' Franquicia Turista page leads with a 76-word legal definition
(deferrable) and omits the allowance (needed now) — the reverse of this rule.

---

### H6 Cohesion (unify) ↔ H4 Progressive discovery (split)

*Minimize fragmentation — keep a task's information together* vs. *present information
in parts*.

| Lean H6 — unify | Lean H4 — split |
|---|---|
| Information is currently spread across pages, tools or apps | Information is currently all on one crowded screen |
| The person must compare or combine the parts | The parts are used one at a time |

**Satisfies both:** **one place, in parts** — unify the *source* (a single page or
surface for the task), chunk the *presentation* (tabs, sections, disclosure within it).

**Example:** Aduanas' Envíos Postales hub — twelve tiles in one place, each opening one
topic. Unified source, chunked presentation.

---

### H5 Control ↔ H3 Assistance (every option is a decision)

*Offer choices* vs. *every setting is one more decision to make*.

| Lean H5 — offer the choice | Lean H3 — decide for them |
|---|---|
| People genuinely differ (motion, density, views, notifications) | Nearly everyone wants the same thing |
| Set once, used often | Chosen every time |

**Satisfies both:** a good default, the option reachable **in context** (not buried in
settings), and the choice remembered.

---

## Writing the Tensions section

Two sentences at most:

> **H3 Assistance ↔ H4 Progressive discovery.** H3 wins here — it's a one-off submission
> to an authority, so predicting the next step matters more than seeing less. Keep H4 by
> listing every step but opening only the current one.

Delete the section if no tension arises.
