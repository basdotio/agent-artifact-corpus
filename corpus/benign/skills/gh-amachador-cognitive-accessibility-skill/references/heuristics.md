# The 9 heuristics

Each states: **Why** (the challenge) · **Do** (the action) · **Benefit** (the effect) ·
**Demands** (what it relieves).

**On wording.** The *Why* statements say "a neurodivergent person may…" — never
"neurodivergent people are…". Neurodivergence covers very different profiles, and two
people can need opposite things (less stimulus / more of it to register). That is why the
answer is so often *control* rather than a new fixed default. The heuristics were
formulated from research with autistic adults of working age (see Source) and are stated
here for the broader population they serve.

## Cognitive demands

Use these to say *who a finding hurts and how*. One element taxing several at once is
grounds to escalate severity.

| Demand | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Focus** | Holding attention; resisting interruption; the inverse — hyperfocus, losing track of time and body state. |
| **Memory** | Holding state, syntax, locations, prior steps. Relieved by recognition over recall. |
| **Learning** | Building the model of how a tool works — and rebuilding it after a change. |
| **Decision-making** | Choosing; paralysis under too many, too vague, or too consequential options. |
| **Communication** | Asking for help, initiating contact, synchronous interaction, tone. |
| **Masking** | Effort spent hiding confusion, distress or a mistake instead of doing the task. |

---

## H1. Purposeful sensory stimulus · *Estímulo sensorial con propósito*

**Why** — A neurodivergent person may be susceptible to sensory stimuli, or may need greater
intensity to register them. Either way, a fixed level is wrong for someone.
**Do** — Let people regulate the amount and type of stimulus. Use stimulus
intentionally, so intensity signals importance.
**Benefit** — Prevents overload; makes salience mean something.

- Reduce and allow control over notifications and interruptions.
- Be intentional with audio and volume.
- Allow configuring on-screen colors — elements and backgrounds.
- Provide a dark mode.
- Be intentional with the number of elements on screen and the layout.
- Be intentional with animation; allow pausing or disabling. Prefer fades over movement.
- Be intentional with graphics and alerts — especially pop-ups.
- Allow disabling video autoplay (especially with sound) and pausing looping media.
- Prefer visuals that clarify ambiguity and support recognition over memory.

**Demands:** Focus · Memory · Decision-making

---

## H2. Clarity and straightforwardness · *Claridad y honestidad*

**Why** — A neurodivergent person may have difficulty catching connotation and indirect
meaning. Depending on emotional intensity, this generates anxiety and pulls focus off
the task.
**Do** — Use explicit, direct language. No assumptions, no metaphor.
**Benefit** — The task gets done, with less effort spent decoding intent.

- Texts simple, understandable, succinct.
- Direct instructions; state expectations and expected results up front.
- Prefer active voice.
- Label icons.
- Clear feedback — errors, confirmations and support text. Positive reinforcement on
  success.
- Use emphasis (bold, italic) to mark what matters.
- Discard figurative, metaphorical and abstract language in the options presented.
- Discard deceptive, condescending or manipulative language in CTAs and options.
- Discard ambiguous language in CTAs and options.

**Demands:** Focus · Learning · Decision-making

---

## H3. Assistance · *Asistencia y guía*

**Why** — A confusing process with ambiguous actions disorients. Control and
predictability mitigate anxiety.
**Do** — Design simply and explicitly. Emphasize the steps and their results.
**Benefit** — Less anxiety, more productivity — and the person can act without asking
for help, which they may be uncomfortable doing.

- Prefer interfaces that favor prediction and show, visually, the exact steps and what
  each expects.
- Offer guides, templates and recommendations at the first step of creation — a new
  document, a prompt.
- Provide several ways to navigate and find things.
- When someone errs, explain it and give the steps to correct it.
- Let people leave a descriptive note to make resuming easier.

**Demands:** Focus · Learning · Decision-making

---

## H4. Progressive discovery · *Descubrimiento progresivo*

**Why** — Tolerance for information overload varies. Arriving in parts eases processing
and avoids context switching and forced multitasking.
**Do** — Present information in parts or sequences. Prefer gradual change.
**Benefit** — Easier processing, less context switching.

- For large amounts of information, disclose progressively — small portions at a time.
- Prefer pagination over infinite scroll: it gives orientation and conveys total
  quantity.
- Provide simple, explicit, preferably visual instructions (tutorials, coachmarks) —
  and make them **skippable** as understanding grows.
- For a redesign, roll out gradually and provide a way back to the previous version,
  even temporarily.

**Demands:** Focus · Learning · Decision-making

---

## H5. Control and customization · *Control y personalización*

**Why** — A neurodivergent person may need more flexibility to view and interact with
information — not only for comfort, but from affinity with systems formed in their own
mental model.
**Do** — Enable different ways of processing information through control of the
interface.
**Benefit** — Easier comprehension; more efficiency.

- Prefer asynchronous, written communication with visual support. If synchronous is
  unavoidable, give as much notice as possible.
- Allow separating aspects of one's operation (separate accounts, profiles).
- Offer different ways to manage on-screen elements (folders, groups, virtual desktops).
- Offer customizable, **persistent** filters and sorting.
- Offer different views of the same information — lists, cards, boards — for different
  contexts and tasks.

**Demands:** Memory · Learning · Decision-making

---

## H6. Cohesion · *Cohesión*

**Why** — A neurodivergent person may have a strong tendency to find systems and link
concepts. A logical hierarchy in familiar terms improves autonomy substantially.
**Do** — Establish a logical hierarchy that minimizes fragmentation and matches familiar
concepts.
**Benefit** — Autonomy, effectiveness, and a sense of security in the system.

- Set visual design principles that order content and favor prediction, learning and
  findability.
- Prioritize an information architecture matching users' mental models.
- Review naming and how functions and pages relate, to favor prediction and findability.
- Use hierarchies and navigation that stay consistent across the whole experience.
- Prefer interfaces that unify communication and minimize fragmentation of what a task
  needs.

**Demands:** Memory · Learning · Decision-making

---

## H7. Consistency and standards · *Consistencia y estándares*

**Why** — A neurodivergent person may have well-established routines, including how they use
other tools. Standard elements provide control and predictability, and let familiar
concepts transfer from known applications.
**Do** — Use standardized components, or ones found in other experiences.
**Benefit** — Predictable results from prior experience; less stress, more autonomy,
muscle memory preserved.

- Prefer patterns and systems already familiar, with predictable results.
- Adopt design systems and UI kits rather than reinventing interface elements.
- Prefer standard naming and proven interaction and navigation patterns (**Jakob's
  Law**).
- Prefer familiar iconography and color that behave as they do elsewhere.
- Define standards for communication and access to information.

**Demands:** Memory · Learning · Decision-making

---

## H8. Resilience and error tolerance · *Resiliencia y tolerancia al error*

**Why** — A neurodivergent person may be uncomfortable asking for help, especially under
urgency. Depending on emotional intensity and hyperfocus, errors can cause overload or a
drop in productivity.
**Do** — Make errors fast and precise to understand; provide recovery and a way to
continue.
**Benefit** — Security and autonomy; anxiety mitigated; no need to ask for help under
pressure.

- Give descriptions and next steps after an error or a rejected input.
- Emphasize that it is the program's error, not the person's failure.
- Explain errors simply but precisely — especially the solvable ones.
- Offer partial/fuzzy matching in filter and search; also allow exact matching.
- Confirm any destructive or permanent change, or one that involves or notifies others.
- Give the time needed to read, decide and act. Discard countdowns on non-urgent,
  deferrable actions.
- Prefer autosave and synchronization.

**Demands:** Memory · Masking · Communication · Learning · Decision-making

---

## H9. Focus management · *Gestión del enfoque*

**Why** — A neurodivergent person may struggle to focus on a task or, conversely, focus so
completely as not to perceive their bodily state or external stimuli.
**Do** — Provide mechanisms to manage concentration, hyperfocus and time.
**Benefit** — Lower cognitive load, burnout prevention, physiological wellbeing.

- Make remaining time visible for tasks and meetings.
- Allow configuring focus/DND granularly — by app, system, schedule, place or person.
- Add friendly proactive prompts to take breaks.
- Use stimulus strategically to hold attention on the parts of the task needing action.
- Use stimulus with purpose and caution, to avoid overload and unintended distraction.
- Set consistent alert levels across sensory channels, mapped to urgency or importance.

**Demands:** Focus · Memory · Decision-making

---

**Complementary, not a replacement.** Applying these does not substitute for WCAG
conformance or conventional usability evaluation.
