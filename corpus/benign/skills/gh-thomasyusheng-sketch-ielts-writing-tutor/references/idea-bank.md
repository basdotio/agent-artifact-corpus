# Topic Idea Bank

Ten topic modules covering the overwhelming majority of Task 2 prompts. Each gives the
sub-questions that actually appear, arguments on both sides, and the vocabulary that
belongs to that topic.

**How to use it.** Identify the topic, read *both* sides, pick **two or three** angles
and develop them properly. Listing six angles in one paragraph produces the
"mentioned but not supported" profile that holds an essay at Band 6.

**Tiers.** Topics 1–7 are the highest-frequency and repay memorising first. Topics
8–10 appear regularly but less often.

## 1. Culture

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Should traditional culture be preserved in a modernising society, or has it had its day?
- Is globalisation making national cultures more alike?
- Should governments fund the arts — museums, theatres, galleries?
- International tourism: does it help or harm local culture?
- Should museums and galleries be free to enter?

**In favour / positive angles**

- Cultural identity underpins social cohesion — *cultural identity · sense of belonging*
- Heritage is irreplaceable: once lost, it cannot be recovered — *irreplaceable heritage*
- Diversity has value in itself and resists homogenisation — *cultural diversity*
- The arts enrich individual life and build national influence — *soft power · enrich the mind*
- Traditional culture generates tourism income — *tourism revenue*

**Against / cost angles**

- Some traditions are outdated or actively harmful — *outdated / harmful customs*
- Public funds are finite; healthcare and welfare come first — *limited public funds*
- Development and preservation sometimes genuinely conflict — *trade-off with development*
- The arts can fund themselves through the market — *self-financing · market-driven*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| cultural heritage | inherited traditions and artefacts |
| cultural identity | what a group understands itself to be |
| cultural homogenization | cultures becoming alike |
| preservation / preserve | keeping something intact |
| indigenous | native to a place |
| intangible heritage | non-physical heritage (music, craft, ritual) |
| authenticity | being genuine rather than staged |
| assimilation | absorption into a dominant culture |
| patronage | financial support for the arts |
| erode / dilute | wear away / weaken |
| revive / revitalize | bring back to life |
| soft power | influence through culture, not force |

## 2. Technology

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Does technology isolate people or connect them?
- Is AI and automation replacing human labour a good or bad thing?
- Are people too dependent on technology?
- Online versus in-person (shopping, learning, socialising)
- Is technological progress worth the loss of privacy?

**In favour / positive angles**

- Productivity gains free people from routine labour — *boost productivity · efficiency*
- Geography stops being a barrier to information and services — *break down barriers · access*
- Medical and scientific breakthroughs extend and improve life — *medical breakthroughs*
- Instant contact sustains relationships across distance — *instant connectivity*

**Against / cost angles**

- Automation displaces workers and creates structural unemployment — *job displacement*
- The digital divide widens existing inequality — *digital divide*
- Surveillance and data misuse erode privacy — *surveillance · data misuse*
- Over-reliance weakens both social skills and basic competences — *over-reliance · social isolation*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| automation | machines doing work people used to do |
| artificial intelligence | machine systems performing cognitive tasks |
| digital divide | gap between those with and without access |
| surveillance | systematic monitoring |
| innovation | new methods or products |
| cutting-edge | most advanced |
| obsolete | superseded, no longer used |
| data privacy | control over personal information |
| dependency / over-reliance | needing something excessively |
| disruptive technology | technology that upends an existing industry |
| streamline | make a process simpler and faster |
| dehumanize | strip of human qualities |

## 3. Media

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Media influence on young people (violence, body image)
- Is celebrity culture a bad influence?
- Does advertising manipulate consumers?
- Fake news and media bias — how damaging are they?
- Should the news report bad events or good ones?

**In favour / positive angles**

- Information spreads quickly and educates the public — *disseminate information*
- A free press holds power to account and protects democracy — *hold power accountable · watchdog*
- Shared coverage gives a society common reference points — *shared experience*

**Against / cost angles**

- Outlets exaggerate for attention — *sensationalism · clickbait*
- Misinformation spreads and polarises — *misinformation · polarization*
- Media reinforces stereotypes and unrealistic standards — *unrealistic standards · stereotypes*
- Advertising manufactures wants people did not have — *manufactured demand*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| mass media | media reaching large audiences |
| misinformation | false information, spread regardless of intent |
| sensationalism | shocking presentation for attention |
| censorship | official suppression of content |
| bias | systematic slant |
| propaganda | material pushing a political line |
| celebrity culture | public preoccupation with famous people |
| media literacy | ability to evaluate media critically |
| tabloid | popular paper focused on scandal |
| coverage | how much and how a story is reported |
| manipulate | influence unfairly or deviously |
| scrutiny | close critical examination |

## 4. Sport and Health

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Should governments invest in promoting sport for everyone?
- Should junk food or sugary drinks be taxed?
- Prevention or treatment — where should healthcare money go?
- Are professional athletes' salaries justified?
- Is health an individual responsibility or the government's?

**In favour / positive angles**

- Prevention costs less than treatment over a lifetime — *preventive healthcare · cost-saving*
- Exercise improves physical and mental health and productivity — *physical & mental well-being*
- Taxation discourages harmful consumption and funds health programmes — *deterrent · fund health programs*
- Sport builds community and discipline — *social cohesion · discipline*

**Against / cost angles**

- Diet is a personal choice the state should not police — *personal freedom · nanny state*
- Consumption taxes fall hardest on low earners — *regressive tax*
- Health outcomes largely follow individual behaviour — *individual responsibility*
- High salaries reflect scarcity and market demand — *market value · scarcity*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| sedentary lifestyle | a life with little physical activity |
| obesity | medically excessive body weight |
| preventive healthcare | care aimed at stopping illness arising |
| well-being | general state of health and contentment |
| life expectancy | average lifespan |
| chronic disease | long-term illness |
| physical activity | bodily exercise of any kind |
| subsidize | support financially |
| junk food | energy-dense, low-nutrient food |
| public health | population-level health |
| deterrent | something that discourages an action |
| nanny state | (critical) a state seen as over-regulating personal life |

## 5. Education

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Should university education be free or state-funded?
- Can online learning replace the classroom?
- Exam-driven teaching versus critical thinking
- Liberal / general education versus vocational training
- Is strict discipline in schools appropriate?
- Is a child's education mainly the school's job or the family's?

**In favour / positive angles**

- Education drives social mobility; free access widens it — *social mobility · equal opportunity*
- Thinking critically outlasts memorised content — *critical thinking vs rote learning*
- Online study is flexible, reaches more people, costs less — *flexible & accessible*
- A broad education produces adaptable people — *holistic · well-rounded*

**Against / cost angles**

- Free tuition strains budgets, and not everyone needs a degree — *strain public funds*
- Online study lacks interaction and demands self-discipline many lack — *lack of interaction · self-discipline*
- Vocational skills translate more directly into employment — *employability · practical skills*
- Weak discipline undermines the learning environment — *lack of discipline*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| curriculum | what is taught in a course |
| rote learning | memorising without understanding |
| critical thinking | reasoned evaluation of claims |
| tuition fees | charges for teaching |
| vocational | job-skills oriented |
| lifelong learning | continued study through adult life |
| social mobility | movement between social classes |
| holistic development | development of the whole person |
| discipline | order and self-control |
| literacy / numeracy | reading-writing / arithmetic ability |
| well-rounded | broadly developed |
| academic | scholarly rather than practical |

## 6. Environment and Development

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Economic growth versus environmental protection — how to choose?
- Are environmental problems for individuals or governments and corporations to solve?
- Should we invest in renewable energy despite the cost?
- What is consumerism doing to the environment?
- Is protecting endangered species worth the money?

**In favour / positive angles**

- Sustainability preserves resources for later generations — *sustainability · future generations*
- Renewables cut pollution and create green jobs — *renewable energy · green jobs*
- Only regulation and corporate obligation operate at scale — *regulation · corporate responsibility*
- Ecosystems and biodiversity have intrinsic value — *biodiversity · ecosystem*

**Against / cost angles**

- Developing economies must address poverty and jobs first — *economic growth · poverty*
- Renewables carry high upfront costs and immature technology — *high upfront cost*
- Individual behaviour barely shifts a structural problem — *limited individual impact*
- Conservation budgets compete with human welfare — *competing priorities*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| sustainability | capacity to continue without depleting resources |
| carbon emissions | greenhouse gases released |
| renewable energy | energy from replenishing sources |
| conservation | protection of nature |
| deforestation | clearing of forests |
| environmental degradation | deterioration of the environment |
| finite resources | resources in limited supply |
| offset | compensate for (emissions) |
| biodiversity | variety of living things |
| ecosystem | an interdependent biological community |
| carbon footprint | total emissions attributable to an actor |
| depletion | exhaustion of a resource |

## 7. Young People and Family Life

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Should parents be strict or give children freedom?
- Grandparents / childcare versus parents raising children themselves
- Family or peers — which shapes a child more?
- Causes of, and responses to, youth crime
- The effect on children when both parents work
- Are only children more selfish?

**In favour / positive angles**

- Parental values and presence form character — *upbringing · formative years*
- Reasonable discipline teaches responsibility and limits — *discipline · boundaries*
- A stable home reduces adolescent problems — *stable environment · delinquency*
- Modelling behaviour works where lecturing does not — *role model · lead by example*

**Against / cost angles**

- Overprotection stunts independence and resilience — *overprotection · resilience*
- Peers and social media increasingly outweigh family — *peer pressure*
- Two incomes buy better material and educational resources — *financial security*
- Temperament is partly innate, not purely taught — *nature vs nurture*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| upbringing | how a child is raised |
| nurture | the environmental side of development |
| peer pressure | influence from one's age group |
| discipline | training in self-control |
| formative years | the period when character forms |
| role model | someone whose behaviour is copied |
| quality time | attentive time spent together |
| generation gap | difference in outlook between age groups |
| adolescence | the teenage years |
| delinquency | minor youth offending |
| resilience | ability to recover from difficulty |
| nature vs nurture | innate traits versus environment |

## 8. Work and Careers

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Remote work or the office?
- One career for life, or changing jobs often?
- Should people pursue salary or satisfaction?
- Should the retirement age rise, or be abolished?
- Employment after machines take over routine work
- Equality for women in the workplace

**In favour / positive angles**

- Remote work adds flexibility and balance — *flexibility · work-life balance*
- Moving between jobs builds a broader skill set — *diverse skills · adaptability*
- Work people care about sustains long-term commitment — *job satisfaction · fulfilment*
- Later retirement retains experience and eases labour shortages — *experience · labour shortage*

**Against / cost angles**

- Remote work isolates people and weakens collaboration — *isolation · weaker collaboration*
- Frequent moves prevent depth and stability — *lack of stability · job-hopping*
- Pay provides security, which is not a trivial consideration — *financial security*
- Older workers staying on can block younger entrants — *block opportunities for youth*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| job satisfaction | contentment with one's work |
| work-life balance | division between work and personal life |
| remote working / telecommuting | working away from the employer's premises |
| career progression | advancement through roles |
| redundancy | job loss because the role ends |
| workforce | all the people employed |
| burnout | exhaustion from prolonged work stress |
| job security | confidence of keeping one's job |
| fulfilment | sense of achievement |
| labour shortage | too few workers for the jobs available |
| morale | collective confidence and spirit |
| expertise | deep specialist skill |

## 9. Crime and Society

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Imprisonment or rehabilitation — which works?
- Should capital punishment be kept or abolished?
- Is crime caused by poverty, or by individual choice?
- How should youth offending be handled?
- Do more police and more surveillance reduce crime?
- Should offenders' identities be published?

**In favour / positive angles**

- Rehabilitation cuts reoffending and eases return to society — *rehabilitation · reintegration*
- Only addressing poverty and education treats the cause — *address root causes*
- Severe penalties deter would-be offenders — *deterrent effect*
- Community programmes prevent what punishment only answers — *prevention over punishment*

**Against / cost angles**

- Imprisonment protects the public from dangerous individuals — *public safety · incapacitation*
- Excessive leniency is unjust to victims — *justice for victims*
- Capital punishment is irreversible and error-prone — *irreversible · wrongful conviction*
- People remain accountable for their own choices — *personal accountability*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| deterrent | something that discourages offending |
| rehabilitation | reform of an offender |
| recidivism / reoffend | committing crime again |
| incarceration | imprisonment |
| root cause | underlying source of a problem |
| capital punishment | the death penalty |
| law enforcement | police and related agencies |
| crime rate | offences per unit of population |
| offender | someone who commits a crime |
| reintegration | return to normal social life |
| deterrence | the effect of discouraging offending |
| accountability | being answerable for one's actions |

## 10. Cities and Infrastructure

**Sub-questions that appear**

- Invest in public transport, or in roads and private cars?
- The advantages and drawbacks of urbanisation
- How can the gap between cities and rural areas be narrowed?
- Should more public space — parks, libraries — be built?
- How should congestion and housing be tackled?
- High-rise or low-rise urban planning?

**In favour / positive angles**

- Public transport cuts congestion and emissions and serves more people — *reduce congestion / carbon*
- Cities concentrate resources and generate jobs — *economies of scale · jobs*
- Public space raises quality of life and social contact — *quality of life · social interaction*
- Good planning makes a city sustainable and liveable — *sustainable · liveable city*

**Against / cost angles**

- Infrastructure is expensive and crowds out other spending — *huge investment · opportunity cost*
- Urbanisation brings overcrowding and pollution — *overcrowding · pollution*
- Expansion consumes green space and farmland — *urban sprawl*
- Housing costs rise beyond ordinary means — *unaffordable housing*

| Word / phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| urbanization | movement of population into cities |
| public transport | shared transport systems |
| congestion | traffic overcrowding |
| infrastructure | roads, utilities, public facilities |
| urban sprawl | uncontrolled outward city growth |
| overcrowding | too many people for the space |
| amenities | useful local facilities |
| sustainable development | growth that does not exhaust resources |
| commute | travel regularly to work |
| affordable housing | housing within ordinary means |
| liveable | pleasant to live in |
| opportunity cost | what is given up by choosing one option |

## Using the vocabulary without losing marks

- **A precise common word beats a misused rare one.** Band 7 credits less common
  vocabulary *used correctly*; a showy word in the wrong collocation costs more than
  the plain word would have.
- **Check the word class before inserting a phrase.** `sustainability` is a noun,
  `sustainable` an adjective, `sustain` a verb. Swapping them is a word-formation
  error charged to Lexical Resource.
- **Watch the uncountables.** `information` · `advice` · `knowledge` · `equipment` ·
  `traffic` · `feedback` · `behaviour` · `work` · `research` · `infrastructure` —
  none of these takes a plural `-s` in this register.
- **Three or four topic words used well is the target.** An essay stuffed with every
  term on a list reads as memorised, and examiners mark memorised language down.
