# Task 1 — Structures by Visual Type

Identify the visual type first. Each one has different Task Achievement requirements,
and marking a process diagram as though it were a line graph produces wrong feedback.

| The visual shows | Type | Section |
|---|---|---|
| A time axis with continuous lines | Line graph | [1](#1-line-graph) |
| Bars comparing categories | Bar chart | [2](#2-bar-chart) |
| Circles divided into segments, usually 2+ | Pie chart | [3](#3-pie-chart) |
| A grid of rows and columns of figures | Table | [4](#4-table) |
| Arrows connecting stages of "how X is made/happens" | Process diagram | [5](#5-process-diagram) |
| Two site plans comparing "before" and "after" | Map / plan | [6](#6-map--plan) |
| Two or more different visual types together | Mixed | [7](#7-mixed-visuals) |

---

## The universal skeleton

Four paragraphs, every time. Decide the structure before writing sentences.

1. **Paraphrase the prompt** (1 sentence) — reword, don't copy.
2. **Overview** (2–3 sentences, immediately after the paraphrase) — the 2–3 most
   striking features only. **No specific figures. No speculation about causes.**
3. **Detail paragraph 1** — grouped by period, category, or similarity, with figures.
4. **Detail paragraph 2** — the remaining group.

### Overview check — all four must be true

- [ ] Contains no specific figures
- [ ] Names only the 2–3 most striking features, not everything
- [ ] Contains no guesses about *why* the data looks like this
- [ ] Sits immediately after the paraphrase, not at the end as a "conclusion"

### Linking toolkit

**Explicit** — cause `As a result` · example `For example` · contrast `while`,
`By contrast` · similarity `Similarly` · concession `although` · condition `If` ·
definition `This means that` · narrowing `Specifically`

**Implicit** — pronoun reference (`They`), a sentence-opening `This` summarising the
previous clause, demonstrative + noun (`these figures`), or a concrete synonym
(`city dwellers` for `urban areas`)

Band 7 mixes both. Relying on explicit connectors alone reads mechanical.

## 1. Line graph

**Paraphrase**
> The line graph **illustrates / shows / compares** [what] **from** [year A] **to** [year B].

**Overview**
> Overall, [X] showed an overall **upward / downward** trend over the period, while [Y]
> **remained relatively stable / fluctuated**. The most notable feature is that [A]
> **overtook** [B] around [approximate point].

**Five interchangeable core patterns**

1. `X + trend verb + adverb + in [year]` — *The number of X rose sharply in 2015.*
2. `There was a + adj + trend noun + in X` — *There was a rapid increase in X.*
3. `[Year] + saw / witnessed a + adj + trend noun` — *2015 witnessed a dramatic rise in X.*
4. `A + adj + trend noun + occurred` — *A sharp rise in X occurred in 2015.*
5. `X + showed a(n) + adj + upward / downward trend`

**Vocabulary**

| Direction | Verbs | Nouns | Adverbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up | rise, increase, grow, climb, surge | rise, increase, growth, surge | sharply, dramatically, significantly (large) · slightly, marginally (small) · steadily (consistent) |
| Down | fall, decline, drop, decrease, plunge | fall, decline, drop, decrease | as above |
| Flat | remain stable, level off, fluctuate | stability, fluctuation | — |

**Joining data points** — present participle (`starting at 20 and rising to 40`) ·
time conjunction (`Before falling in 2010, X had peaked`) · coordination
(`X rose steadily, but Y fluctuated`) · past participle (`followed by a slight
decline`) · relative clause (`…, when it began to level off`) · time adverb
(`After this, the figure remained unchanged`)

**Before writing, check:** the maximum value on the Y-axis, and which years actually
appear on the X-axis. A figure beyond the axis range, or a year not on the chart, is
fabrication rather than imprecision.

## 2. Bar chart

If the bars run across time, treat it as a line graph. If they compare categories,
group by one dimension and order within each group from largest to smallest.

**Paraphrase**
> The bar chart **compares** [what] **across / among** [categories] **in** [year/place].

**Overview**
> Overall, [A] had the **highest / largest** [X], whereas [B] recorded the **lowest**.

**Three comparison patterns**

- `A is [X] times more / higher than B.`
- `A produced [X] times more Y than B did.`
- `There was [X] times more A than B.`

Also: `twice / three times as high as` · `X% higher than` · `accounted for X% of the
total` · `the second highest, followed by…` · `by far the highest`

**Before writing, check:** your multiples. Re-do each ratio once — a miscalculated
multiple is a Task Achievement error no amount of good prose recovers.

---

## 3. Pie chart

Usually two or more charts. The overview **must compare across them**, not describe
each in turn.

**Paraphrase**
> The pie charts **compare** the proportion of [what] **in** [A] and [B].

**Overview**
> Overall, [category] made up the largest proportion in both, although [A] relied
> considerably more on [category] than [B] did.

**Patterns** — `The majority / minority of X…, with [figure]` · `X accounted for /
made up [figure]` · `There was [figure] of X, which…` · `For [topic], [figure] of X…` ·
`X constituted the largest / smallest share of…`

**Before writing, check:** that each shading or colour is matched to the right legend
entry, and that proportions sum to roughly 100%. Mismatching a legend entry reverses
a whole trend and is one of the costliest Task 1 errors.

---

## 4. Table

Tables carry the most data and reward selection most heavily. Do not try to report
every cell.

**Paraphrase**
> The table **shows / provides information about** [what] **in** [categories]
> **during** [period].

**Overview**
> Overall, [A] had consistently **higher / lower** [X] than the others, while
> [shared pattern or clearest exception] across all categories.

**Organising** — group by whichever axis puts similar figures together · lead with the
extremes, then the middle · use `the figure for…` (anything), `the number of…`
(countable plural), `the amount of…` (uncountable)

**Patterns** — `X had the highest figure for Y, at [figure].` · `In terms of Y, X
ranked first among the five.` · `A similar pattern is seen in X and Y, both of
which…` · `By contrast, Z recorded a markedly different figure.`

## 5. Process diagram

Decide first whether the process is **cyclical** or **linear** — and say which in the
overview.

**Paraphrase**
> The diagram **illustrates the process of** [X] **/ how** [X] **is produced**.

**Overview**
> Overall, the process involves [number] main stages, beginning with [first] and
> ending with / returning to [last].

**Core grammar — the passive dominates.** The interest is in what happens to the
material, not who does it.

- `First, [material] **is** [verb-ed].`
- `Once X **has been** [verb-ed], it is then [verb-ed].`
- `This **is followed by** + noun / gerund`
- `**After being** [verb-ed], X is [verb-ed].`
- `Subsequently / Next / After that, …`
- `Finally, X **is** [verb-ed], and the process **is complete**.`
- Cyclical close: `The cycle then begins again when…`

**Before writing, check:** that every labelled stage appears (skipping one the diagram
names is a reported omission), and — for a cycle — that your final sentence actually
closes the loop back to the start. Stopping mid-cycle is the classic failure on this
type.

## 6. Map / plan

Two plans, "before" and "after". The task is what changed — **and what didn't**.

**Paraphrase**
> The maps / plans **compare** [place] **in** [year A] **and** [today / year B].

**Overview**
> Overall, the area has been considerably **redeveloped / modernised**, with several
> facilities **relocated or replaced**. The most significant change is that
> [single biggest change].

**Patterns by change type**

| Change | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Demolished / replaced | `X has been replaced by Y.` |
| Repurposed | `X has been converted into Y.` |
| Added | `A new X has been built to the north of Y.` |
| Swapped | `X and Y have swapped positions.` / `X, which used to be next to A, is now next to B.` |
| Enlarged | `X has been extended / widened.` |
| **Unchanged** | `X, however, remains unchanged.` |

**Compass vocabulary** — `to the north / south / east / west of` · `next to` ·
`adjacent to` · `in the centre of` · `along` · `opposite` · `at the edge of`

**Before writing, check:** every position claim against *both* plans rather than from
memory, and every ownership or access change (public → private, open → restricted) for
direction. Reversing one of those states the opposite of the truth.

**Structure suggestion:** one paragraph for water/transport features, one for buildings
and roads, one for the single largest change. Never one undivided block.

## 7. Mixed visuals

The easiest type to half-answer.

**Paraphrase**
> The [chart A] and [chart B] **together provide information about** [topic],
> focusing on [A's focus] and [B's focus] respectively.

**Overview — must cover both.**
> Overall, [A's headline trend], while [B's headline feature].

**Two structures:** one paragraph per visual (when the data sets are unrelated), or
one paragraph per theme spanning both (when they are related — this shows more
analytical control).

**Bridges** — `Meanwhile,` · `Similarly,` · `By contrast,` · `In line with this,` ·
`This is reflected in the [other visual], which shows…`

**Before writing, check:** that the overview names something from *every* visual.
Covering only one is the single most common failure here. If the two data sets look
causally related, state the correspondence — but never assert cause.

---

## Final check, any type

- [ ] Overview sits after the paraphrase, carries no figures, guesses no causes
- [ ] The response is paragraphed (overview + at least two detail paragraphs)
- [ ] Every direction, position and ownership claim was re-checked against the visual
- [ ] Subject–verb agreement checked sentence by sentence
- [ ] No sentence explains *why* — Task 1 describes only
