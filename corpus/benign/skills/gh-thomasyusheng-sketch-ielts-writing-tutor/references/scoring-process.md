# The Six-Step Scoring Process

Run this **per criterion**, in order, every time. A band awarded without completing
these steps is not a band — it is a guess.

---

## Step 1 — List concrete problems, and count them

Write out each problem you actually found, quoting the candidate's own words.

- Quote the original. `"the amount of works done"`, not "some noun errors".
- Count them. The number feeds Step 3.
- **No listed items means no band for that criterion.** If you cannot name three
  specific problems, you cannot justify a 5.

Adjectives are not evidence. "Weak vocabulary" is not a finding; `"economy ability"`
used four times where `financial` was meant is a finding.

---

## Step 2 — The communication threshold test

This single step decides the 5 / 5.5 boundary, and it is where nearly all harsh
grading goes wrong. Ask one binary question about the problems you listed:

> **Do these make the reader misunderstand, misread, or have to re-read to follow?**

- **Yes** → 5 or below is available.
- **No** — the writing is merely unpolished, unidiomatic, thin, or conspicuous →
  **that criterion starts at 5.5.**

The official wording is the authority here. Band 5 turns on errors that
*"cause difficulty for the reader"* / *"may impede communication"*. Band 6 explicitly
tolerates errors that *"do not impede communication"*.

### What this test is not

Three traps, each of which has produced a wrongly harsh band:

| Trap | Reality |
|---|---|
| "This sentence is long and tangled" | Dense ≠ incomprehensible. If it has a working main clause and the point survives one careful read, it does not impede. |
| "A specific detail is wrong" | A wrong figure inside an otherwise correct trend does not stop the reader understanding the trend. |
| "This idea is underdeveloped" | *Thin* is Band 6 territory (`"inadequately developed"`). *Absent* is not. Under-explaining ≠ not answering. |

### Calibrating severity against precedent

Before calling something "impeding", compare it to the worst sentence you have
already judged. A sentence with **no independent clause at all** is a genuine
breakdown. A sentence with a working backbone plus four trailing subordinate clauses
is ugly but survivable — and must not be scored *more* harshly than the first one.
Inconsistency between cases is itself a marking error.

---

## Step 3 — Density sanity-check

Only for criteria that Step 2 cleared as non-impeding.

| Problems found | Indicative band |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | 6 |
| 3–5 | 5.5 |
| 6+ | 5.5 (do not go lower unless Step 2 said *impeding*) |

This is a **calibration aid, not a formula**. If your holistic impression differs from
the table by more than 0.5, something is wrong in one of the two — find out which.
Do not simply take the lower number.

Note the deliberate floor: six errors and twenty errors both land at 5.5 when none of
them impedes communication. Volume alone does not push a criterion to Band 5;
**difficulty for the reader** does.

---

## Step 4 — One problem, one deduction

The same flaw must never be charged to two criteria. Ownership:

| Problem type | Charge to | Never also to |
|---|---|---|
| Thin argument, unbalanced coverage, missing required part | TR / TA | CC |
| Word choice, spelling, word formation | LR | TR |
| Over-long sentences, clumsy punctuation, weak referencing | CC | GRA |
| Real grammar (tense, agreement, prepositions, articles) | GRA | CC |

This trap is subtle in practice. A single tangled sentence usually contains *two
distinct* problems — a cohesion problem (clause boundaries) and a grammar problem
(a broken relative clause). Those are separable and may each be charged once, to
different criteria. What is forbidden is charging *the same* problem twice under two
names.

---

## Step 5 — Interrogate every score below 6

For each criterion you scored under 6, answer out loud:

> **"Why is this not half a band higher? Quote the descriptor."**

- Can cite it (*"Band 6 requires X; this response lacks X"*) → the band holds.
- Can only say *"it doesn't feel good enough"* → **raise it by 0.5.**

### Do not deduct for what the band already tolerates

Descriptors describe what a band *permits*, not only what it demands. Band 7 Task
Response states there *"may be a tendency to overgeneralise"* — so generalisation is
**inside** Band 7 and cannot be the reason for withholding Band 7. Band 6 Coherence
allows cohesion to be *"faulty or mechanical"* — so a comma splice is not, by itself,
a reason to drop below 6.

Using a band's own tolerances as grounds to deny that band is a logical contradiction,
and it is one of the two most common sources of over-harsh marking.

---

## Step 6 — When torn, go higher

If you are genuinely oscillating between two bands, take the higher one. The measured
error direction of this whole process is downward; this step counterweights it.

---

## What this process does not touch

It governs **where the band lands**. It has no authority over **what gets reported**.

Find every error. Quote it. Correct it. Rank it. The deliverable is an accurate band
paired with a complete, unsparing error list — not a kind band paired with a kind
error list.

---

## Two worked calibration failures

Real cases, both caught on review. Both point the same direction.

### Failure 1 — double-charging one sentence

A Task 1 overview sentence with tangled comparison logic was charged to **Task
Achievement** ("faulty comparison") *and* to **Coherence and Cohesion** ("hard to
follow"). One flaw, two deductions, in direct violation of Step 4.

Correct handling: the facts asserted were accurate, so nothing was owed to Task
Achievement; the problem was entirely sentence construction, so it belonged to
Coherence alone. Fixing the double-charge raised the overall band by half a step.

### Failure 2 — deducting for a tolerated feature

A Task 2 essay was held at 6.5 partly because its supporting argument generalised.
But Band 7's own descriptor names overgeneralisation as a permitted characteristic of
Band 7 writing. The deduction used a Band 7 tolerance as a reason to refuse Band 7.

Correct handling: the essay extended and supported both main ideas with concrete
examples, which is what Band 7 actually requires. It scored 7.

---

## Computing the final band

1. Average the four criteria.
2. Round to the nearest half band: `.25` rounds **down**, `.75` rounds **up**.
3. For a full Writing score, weight **Task 1 : Task 2 = 1 : 2**.

Show the arithmetic. If a proposed fix would not change the *rounded* band, say so
plainly instead of overstating its effect — an honest "this cleans up the profile but
the rounded band stays at 6" is worth more than an inflated promise.
