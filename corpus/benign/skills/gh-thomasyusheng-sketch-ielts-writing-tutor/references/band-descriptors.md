# Band Boundaries — A Working Summary

> **Source note.** The official IELTS Writing band descriptors are published free by the
> IELTS partners (British Council / IDP / Cambridge) and are the only authority for
> marking. This file does **not** reproduce them. It is a working summary, in my own
> words, of the distinctions that actually decide borderline cases — written to be used
> *alongside* the official public descriptors, not instead of them.
>
> Get the originals: search "IELTS Writing Task 1 / Task 2 band descriptors public version".

Each task is marked on four criteria, weighted equally at 25% each.

| Task 1 | Task 2 |
|---|---|
| Task Achievement (TA) | Task Response (TR) |
| Coherence and Cohesion (CC) | Coherence and Cohesion (CC) |
| Lexical Resource (LR) | Lexical Resource (LR) |
| Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA) | Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA) |

---

## The boundary that matters most: 5 vs 6

Across every criterion, the same idea separates these two bands:

| | Band 5 | Band 6 |
|---|---|---|
| **Governing idea** | Problems *cause difficulty for the reader* | Problems *do not impede communication* |
| Errors | Frequent; can obstruct | Present, sometimes noticeable, but survivable |
| Ideas | Limited, underdeveloped, possibly no clear position | Relevant, present, may be unevenly developed |
| Organisation | Limited; progression may be unclear | Coherent overall, though cohesion may be mechanical |

So the operative question is never "how many errors?" It is **"does the reader get
there?"** A dense but navigable response sits at 6. A cleaner response that genuinely
obstructs meaning sits at 5.

## Task Response / Task Achievement

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **7** | Every part of the prompt handled; a clear position sustained start to finish; main ideas extended *and supported*. May overgeneralise — that is permitted at this band, not a deduction. |
| **6** | All parts addressed, though some more fully than others. Position present. Relevant ideas, some thin or under-explained. Details may be inaccurate or overlooked. |
| **5** | Only partially addresses the requirements. Position unclear or inconsistent. Ideas limited and undeveloped; comparisons may be faulty. Task 1: may recount detail with no real overview. |

**Deciding 6 vs 7:** are the ideas *supported* (example, mechanism, evidence) or merely
*asserted*? Support is the hinge. A concrete, relevant example that actually carries
the argument is Band 7 behaviour even when the sentences around it are rough.

**Deciding 5 vs 6:** did the response address every part, however unevenly? Answering
one half of a two-part question is a Band 5 signal — that is a missing part, not an
uneven one. An idea that is present but shallow is Band 6.

**Task 1 specifics:** the overview is close to mandatory — it goes directly after the
paraphrase, names 2–3 headline features, and carries no specific figures. Speculating
about causes is off-task. Fabricated data (a value outside the chart's own axis, a year
absent from its own timeline) is heavier than imprecision, and a reversed fact
(reporting "private" as "public") is heavier still.

## Coherence and Cohesion

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **7** | Logical progression throughout; cohesive devices used appropriately, if sometimes over- or under-used; each paragraph has a clear central topic. |
| **6** | Information arranged coherently with overall progression; devices used effectively but cohesion between or inside sentences may be faulty or mechanical; referencing not always clear. |
| **5** | Some organisation but progression may be missing; devices inadequate, repetitive, or misused; paragraphing absent or unhelpful. |

**The common over-deduction:** Band 6 explicitly tolerates *faulty or mechanical*
cohesion. A comma splice, a vague `which`, one over-stuffed sentence — none of these
alone drops a response below 6.

**What genuinely lives at Band 5:** no paragraphing at all; sentences whose clause
boundaries collapse so the reader cannot reconstruct the sequence; a response that
lists features with no discernible ordering principle.

**Paragraphing is assessable in its own right.** A response delivered as one
undifferentiated block is a real deduction even when its internal order is logical —
but it is *one* deduction, not grounds for collapsing the whole criterion.

## Lexical Resource

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **7** | Enough range to be flexible and precise; some less common items and idiom, with occasional inaccuracy; occasional errors in word choice or spelling. |
| **6** | Adequate range for the task; attempts less common vocabulary with some inaccuracy; spelling and word-formation errors present but not impeding. |
| **5** | Limited range, minimally adequate; noticeable spelling or word-formation errors that may cause the reader difficulty. |

**Deciding 5.5 vs 6:** Band 6 openly permits spelling and word-formation errors
provided they do not impede. A run of misspellings is therefore *not* automatically
sub-6 — but a basic word-class confusion repeated four times across one essay
(`economy` used where `financial` is meant) is denser evidence of a gap than scattered
typos, and pulls the criterion down.

**Deciding 6 vs 7:** is the less-common vocabulary doing real work, or decorative? A
precise collocation used correctly earns Band 7 credit; a showy word used wrongly does
not, and may cost more than a plain one.

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **7** | A range of complex structures; frequent error-free sentences; good control, with few errors. |
| **6** | A mix of simple and complex forms; errors in grammar and punctuation occur but rarely reduce communication. |
| **5** | Limited range; complex attempts less accurate than simple ones; frequent errors; punctuation may be faulty; errors can cause the reader difficulty. |

This is the criterion least prone to over-generous marking and least often overturned
on review — trust a careful count here.

**Deciding 5.5 vs 6:** `frequent` is the operative Band 5 word. A high density of
errors that nevertheless never obscures meaning sits at 5.5. Pushing it to 5 requires
Step 2 to have found genuine reader difficulty, not merely a long error list.

**Range counts as well as accuracy.** A response attempting relative clauses,
concessive clauses, passives and conditionals — and getting some wrong — is not the
same as one that only ever writes simple sentences correctly. Credit the attempt,
then charge the inaccuracy.

---

## Weighting and rounding

- Four criteria, equal weight, averaged per task.
- Round to the nearest half band: `.25` down, `.75` up.
- Overall Writing: **Task 1 counts once, Task 2 counts twice.**
