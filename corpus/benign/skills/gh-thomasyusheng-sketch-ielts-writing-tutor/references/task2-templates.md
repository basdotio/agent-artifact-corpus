# Task 2 — The Six Essay Types

Identify the type from the prompt's question words **before** planning. Choosing the
wrong frame is a Task Response failure that no amount of good writing repairs.

| Prompt wording | Type | Section |
|---|---|---|
| `To what extent do you agree or disagree?` | Agree / Disagree | [1](#1-agree--disagree) |
| `Discuss both views and give your own opinion.` | Discuss Both Views | [2](#2-discuss-both-views) |
| `Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?` | Outweigh | [3](#3-advantages-outweigh-disadvantages) |
| `Is this a positive or negative development?` | Positive / Negative | [4](#4-positive-or-negative-development) |
| `What are the causes? What solutions…?` | Problem & Solution | [5](#5-problem--solution) |
| Two distinct questions in one prompt | Two-Part Question | [6](#6-two-part-question) |

**Filling in the frames:** when paraphrasing the prompt, swap the wording, not the
concept. Write the position sentence *before* drafting the body, then check each
paragraph against it — this is what stops an essay switching sides halfway through.

---

## 1. Agree / Disagree

**Introduction** (3 sentences, 40–60 words)

1. *There is an ongoing debate about whether* **[restated claim]**.
2. *Supporters argue that* **[their core reason]**, *while others contend that*
   **[the opposing reason]**.
3. Position — pick one:
   - Full agreement/disagreement: *While* **[their point]** *is not without merit,
     I firmly agree that* **[your position]**.
   - Partial agreement (safer): *While I accept that* **[what you concede]**,
     *I would argue that* **[your main position]**.

**Conclusion** (2 sentences, 35–50 words)

1. *In light of the above, I maintain that* **[restate — reword, don't repeat]**.
2. Close — pick one:
   - *[Government / schools / individuals] should therefore* **[concrete action]**.
   - *Provided that* **[condition]**, **[expected outcome]**.
   - *As* **[trend]** *continues, this issue will only become more significant.*

> The position sentence must say what you *claim*, not what the essay will *do*.
> "This essay will present positive views" states nothing and earns nothing.

## 2. Discuss Both Views

**Introduction**

1. *There is an ongoing debate about whether* **[restated issue]**.
2. *While some believe* **[view A]**, *others maintain that* **[view B]**.
3. *This essay will consider both perspectives before explaining why I believe*
   **[your position]**.

**Conclusion**

1. *In conclusion, although* **[what view A gets right]**, *I maintain that*
   **[your position]**.
2. Close — pick one:
   - *Rather than replacing one another,* **[A]** *and* **[B]** *are likely to*
     **[how they coexist]**.
   - **[Actor]** *should therefore* **[concrete action]**.

> Two requirements this type enforces: pick a side (or state explicitly which
> situations each view fits — "leave it to others to decide" is not a position), and
> give both views comparable space. One view fully developed and the other given two
> sentences is a reported imbalance.

## 3. Advantages Outweigh Disadvantages

**Introduction**

1. *There is an ongoing debate about whether* **[practice]** *brings more benefits
   than drawbacks.*
2. **[Practice]** *undoubtedly* **[main benefit direction]**, *though it also raises
   concerns about* **[main drawback direction]**.
3. Position: *In my view, the advantages of* **[practice]** *clearly outweigh the
   drawbacks.* (or the reverse)

**Conclusion**

1. *In light of the above, I maintain that the benefits of* **[practice]** *outweigh
   its drawbacks.*
2. *Although* **[drawback]** *should not be dismissed,* **[why the benefit weighs
   more]**, *and* **[recommendation or condition]**.

> This type demands an explicit act of weighing. Listing advantages, then listing
> disadvantages, then stopping does not answer the question asked.
>
> Agreement watch: *the advantages … outweigh* (plural) vs *this approach …
> outweighs* (singular).

## 4. Positive or Negative Development

**Introduction**

1. **[Restated trend]** *has become increasingly common in recent years.*
2. *Some regard this as* **[positive reading]**, *while others see it as*
   **[negative reading]**.
3. *Overall, I regard this as a largely* **positive / negative** *development,
   despite* **[one qualifying consideration]**.

**Conclusion**

1. *In conclusion, I maintain that* **[trend]** *is, on balance, a* **positive /
   negative** *development.*
2. *Provided that* **[safeguard or condition]**, **[why the risk stays manageable or
   the benefit durable]**.

> `despite` takes a noun or gerund — *despite the cost*, *despite rising costs*.
> For a full clause use `although`.

## 5. Problem & Solution

**Introduction**

1. **[Restated problem]** *has become a pressing concern in recent years.*
2. *This issue affects* **[who]** *and shows little sign of improving.*
3. *This essay will examine the main causes of this problem before suggesting how it
   might be addressed.*

**Conclusion**

1. *In conclusion,* **[problem]** *is primarily driven by* **[cause 1]** *and*
   **[cause 2]**.
2. *Addressing it will require* **[solution 1]** *and* **[solution 2]**, *though*
   **[condition or who is responsible]**.

> Each solution must answer a cause you actually named. Listing one set of causes and
> an unrelated set of solutions is the standard failure here.
>
> This is a report-type prompt: no personal position is required.

---

## 6. Two-Part Question

**Introduction**

1. **[Restated background]** *raises two important questions.*
2. Name both questions — pick the line that matches what they ask:
   - Neither asks for an opinion: *This essay will first examine* **[Q1]**, *and then
     consider* **[Q2]**.
   - Only the second asks for one: *This essay will first examine* **[Q1]**, *and then
     consider* **[Q2]**, *before arguing that* **[your position on Q2]**.
   - Both ask for one: *This essay will argue that* **[position on Q1]**, *and will
     explain why* **[position on Q2]**.

**Conclusion**

1. *In conclusion,* **[answer to Q1]**.
2. *As for* **[Q2's topic]**, **[answer to Q2]**, *and* **[recommendation or outlook]**.

> This is the type most often half-answered. Name both questions in the introduction
> and give each its own body paragraph.
>
> Words that signal an opinion is wanted: `to what extent` · `do you agree` ·
> `do you think` · `how important` · `how far`. Their absence signals description.

## Six universal angles

When the ideas won't come, walk these in order and ask what each has to say about
the prompt.

| Angle | Ask about | Vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic** | Cost, jobs, industry, public spending, return | economic burden · job creation · cost-effective |
| **Social** | Fairness, cohesion, crime, mobility | social cohesion · inequality · social mobility |
| **Individual** | Freedom, health, development, wellbeing | personal freedom · well-being · self-development |
| **Environmental** | Sustainability, pollution, resources | sustainability · carbon footprint · finite resources |
| **Ethical** | Rights, duties, justice | moral responsibility · rights · fairness |
| **Practical** | Feasibility, side effects, better alternatives | feasibility · side effects · alternative solutions |

Two or three angles developed properly beat six mentioned in passing.

## Universal argumentative vocabulary

| Function | Expressions |
|---|---|
| Stating a position | firmly believe · be convinced that |
| Partial agreement | to some extent · while there is truth in |
| Objective claim | it is widely acknowledged that |
| Cause and effect | stem from · give rise to · result in · contribute to · account for |
| Concession | admittedly · it is true that |
| Counter-move | nevertheless · that said · however |
| Undercutting a view | this view overlooks · fails to account for |
| Introducing an example | a case in point is · this is exemplified by |
| Degree | substantial · considerable · marginal · negligible · overwhelming · disproportionate · profound · far-reaching |
| Contrast | whereas · in contrast to · as opposed to |
| Concluding | all things considered · on balance · in light of the above · I maintain that |

## Thirty-second check before submitting

- [ ] `despite` + noun/gerund; `although` + full clause
- [ ] `not only … but also` joins matching word classes
- [ ] No comma joining two complete sentences — use a full stop, semicolon, or conjunction
- [ ] No doubled connectors (`although … but`, `because … so`)
- [ ] Subject–verb agreement, checked sentence by sentence
- [ ] `less` + uncountable, `fewer` + countable plural
- [ ] The position sentence says what you claim, not what the essay will do
- [ ] Both sides given comparable space (discuss-both-views), or both questions
      answered (two-part)
- [ ] The conclusion introduces no new argument or example
