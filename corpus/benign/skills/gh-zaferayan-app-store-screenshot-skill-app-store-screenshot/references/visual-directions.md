# Visual Directions

Use this reference when choosing a creative direction for framed store screenshots.

## Non-Negotiable Preservation

The raw screenshot must stay intact.

Do not change:

- UI text
- Icons
- Navigation labels
- Charts, balances, prices, totals, or statistics
- Button states
- Images shown inside the app
- Layout, spacing, colors, or typography inside the screenshot

If the generation tool cannot reliably preserve screenshot internals, use a compositing workflow: keep the original screenshot as a placed image inside a device frame, then generate or design only the surrounding scene.

## Choosing a Direction

Base the direction on the screenshot itself:

- Dominant brand colors
- Background color and surface style
- App category
- Typography mood
- Visual density
- Whether the UI is playful, clinical, premium, technical, calm, or energetic

Avoid choosing a color scheme because it is common in app mockups. The final asset should feel derived from the product.

## Background Patterns

Strong default options:

- Soft monochrome field using a brand color
- Subtle radial light behind the device
- Clean editorial backdrop with restrained texture
- Minimal geometric panels inspired by app surfaces
- Abstract product-specific shapes that do not compete with the UI
- Split-tone background when showing multiple phones

Stronger than any of these is a scene that shows what the app is about: artwork for an art app, caption bubbles for a subtitle app, a landscape for an outdoors app. SKILL.md ("Backgrounds") covers how to source or generate one. Thematic decoration is welcome when it stays abstract, low-contrast, and out of the way of the headline and the screen.

The background should frame the product, not become the product.

Avoid:

- Generic neon gradients
- Decoration with no link to the app's subject
- Floating UI cards, fake notifications, or other fake interface elements around the phone
- Busy photos behind the headline, or any image that makes the text hard to read
- Excessive shadows, reflections, or glass effects

## Device Frames

Use a realistic device frame unless the user asks for a frameless crop.

Match platform when possible:

- iOS screenshots: modern iPhone frame
- Android screenshots: modern Android or Pixel-style frame
- Unknown platform: infer from status bar, navigation, and aspect ratio

The phone should feel physical but not heavy. Keep bezels, corner radius, camera area, shadow, and screen fit believable.

## Headlines

Headlines should be short, specific, and feature-led. Prefer 2 to 6 words when possible.

Good headline patterns:

- "Meals, planned fast."
- "Your budget, clear."
- "Sleep trends that stick."
- "Invoices without friction."
- "Focus on what changed."

Avoid:

- Vague claims like "The best app ever"
- Long sentences
- Technical implementation details
- Repeating text already obvious in the screenshot
- Overpromising regulated outcomes in finance, health, or wellness categories

## Typography

Use clean, premium display typography. The headline must remain readable at thumbnail size.

Recommended treatment:

- One strong headline, with the benefit words emphasized
- Optional one-sentence subheadline when the headline alone does not explain the feature
- Optional tiny category label only if it adds clarity
- High contrast against the background
- Enough spacing above or beside the device

Avoid paragraphs, feature lists, or instructional text inside the marketing image unless explicitly requested. A subheadline is one sentence, not a description.

## Composition Patterns

Single screenshot:

- Portrait device centered or slightly lower than center
- Headline above or beside the phone
- Background light focused behind the device
- Enough margin for store cropping

Two screenshots:

- One primary phone, one secondary angled or offset
- Shared headline system
- Preserve legibility of both screens

Three to five screenshots:

- Use a consistent grid or sequence
- Assign one headline per image
- Keep frame size, lighting, and typography consistent
- Vary background accents enough to show progression without breaking the set

## Category Notes

Wellness:

- Calm, airy, tactile, soft contrast
- Avoid clinical sterility unless the app is medical
- Do not imply guaranteed health outcomes

Finance:

- Clear, trustworthy, precise, high contrast
- Avoid fake balances, fake card numbers, or altered figures
- Keep claims conservative

Dark mode:

- Use depth through subtle light, contrast, and restrained color
- Avoid muddy low-contrast backgrounds
- Make the device edge visible

Productivity:

- Crisp, organized, focused
- Use structured backgrounds or subtle workspace cues
- Headlines should emphasize clarity, speed, or control

Education:

- Friendly and structured
- Avoid childish treatment unless the product is for children
- Show progress, lessons, or outcomes plainly

Creator tools:

- More expressive color and motion cues are acceptable
- Keep the actual creative surface inside the app untouched
- Avoid overwhelming the UI with decorative assets
