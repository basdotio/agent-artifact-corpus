# Employee or contractor: what to look for in the evidence

Being told "invoice me with your ABN" does not make someone a contractor. Australian law looks
at the real substance, practical reality and true nature of the relationship (Fair Work Act 2009
s 15AA, inserted by the 2024 "Closing Loopholes" changes), and s 357 prohibits misrepresenting
employment as independent contracting (sham contracting). Whether a given case crosses the line
is a legal judgment: collect the facts below, label them, and let FWO or a lawyer draw the
conclusion.

Verify the current law before quoting section numbers; this note is a map, not a citation.

## Indicators and the evidence that shows them

| Indicator of employment | What it looks like in chat / email | How to label it |
|---|---|---|
| Control over hours | "Would 9:30 be too late?" answered with directions and a phone call; being told what days to come; reporting lateness | "Employer set/approved start time, <date> <time>" |
| Control over the work | Task assignments ("send order #123 to this address"), criticism of how work was done, back-end screenshots sent by the boss | "Employer directed and supervised work, <date>" |
| Integration | Added to the staff chat group, given access to internal systems (sheets, back-end, email), company uniform | "Integrated into business, <date>" |
| Employer's equipment and premises | Worked at their warehouse/shop on their computer; supplied nothing of their own | "No own equipment" |
| Paid by time, not result | Hourly rate, fortnightly invoices that are hours × rate | "Hourly pay" |
| No delegation | Could not send someone else to do the shift | "No right to delegate" |
| No business risk or goodwill | No quote, no profit or loss, no insurance, no other clients, not registered for GST, ABN used only for this job | "No independent business" |
| Expectation of ongoing work | Rosters, "see you Monday" | "Continuing engagement" |
| How both sides spoke about it | The worker calls it "wages"; the boss never corrects them | "Characterised as wages, <date>" |
| Removal after chasing | Removed from the group chat the day payment was chased in writing | "Retaliation after chaser, <date>" |

Indicators of genuine contracting (note them too, honestly): the worker quoted a price, used
their own tools, could send a substitute, worked for several clients, carried insurance,
controlled their own hours.

## What the employer's silence proves

Never replying is not an admission, but it matters: a respondent who never disputed the hours,
the amount, or the characterisation, over months, has a weak position later. Record each chaser
with date, channel and the (non)response, and put the list in the FWO description.

## Records and the burden of proof

Employers must keep time and wage records and issue pay slips. When they have none, s 557C of the
Fair Work Act can reverse the burden in court proceedings for the worker's allegations about
hours and pay. Do not promise this outcome; note it as a point for the adviser, and keep the
worker's own contemporaneous records (invoices, chat timestamps, transport taps) as the primary
proof.

## Superannuation

Unpaid super for an employee (and for some contractors paid mainly for labour) is generally
enforced by the ATO, not FWO. Mention it in the FWO enquiry so the award shortfall is complete,
and tell the user they may need a separate ATO "unpaid super" report.
