# Rule of six

## Rules

- Judge a cut by six criteria in this order: emotion, story, rhythm, eye trace, two-dimensional plane of the screen, three-dimensional space of action.
- When a cut cannot satisfy all six, give up from the bottom: break spatial continuity before rhythm, rhythm before story, never emotion. A cut that is right for the feeling beats one that matches perfectly.
- Emotion: what should the audience feel at this frame? Cut to the face that carries it.
- Story: does the cut move the story forward or answer a question the audience has?
- Rhythm: the cut falls on a beat the scene has set (a breath, a step, a line end), not at a fixed interval.
- Eye trace: put the point of interest of the incoming shot near where the eye was in the outgoing shot; a jump across the frame costs a moment of search.
- 2D plane: screen direction and eyelines hold across the cut (the axis; cinewright-continuity checks the plan).
- 3D space: positions in the room stay where the audience placed them.
- For generated takes: a take that is wrong on the bottom two (a prop moved, a hand on the other side) can stay if it is right on the top three; a take wrong on emotion or story is re-rendered, not cut around.

## Numbers

- Murch's weights: emotion 51%, story 23%, rhythm 10%, eye trace 7%, 2D plane 5%, 3D space 4%. His figures express priority, not a scoring formula.

## Pitfalls

- Choosing takes by technical cleanliness alone gives a smooth, lifeless cut.
- Matching every cut on continuity first: the habit of a continuity check, not of an editor.
