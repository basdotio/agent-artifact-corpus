# Cutting on action

## Rules

- Cut while something moves (a hand reaching, a head turning, a door opening) and pick the motion up in the next shot. Motion pulls the eye across the cut and hides small mismatches of position or light.
- Cut about a third of the way into the movement: the start announces it, the rest completes in the new shot.
- Repeat 2-4 frames of the motion across the cut (an overlap), not zero; a perfectly continuous cut often looks like it skipped.
- The card pair must plan it: card A ends mid-motion (`end_state` names the motion in progress), card B starts with the same motion (`start_state`). cinewright-movement, entry one-phrase-per-shot, writes the phrase; this entry cuts it.
- Generated motion speeds differ between takes. If the incoming take is slower or faster, cut later in the slow one so the motion speed matches at the cut.
- Never cut on a still frame between two static shots of the same subject at similar size: that is a jump cut.

## Numbers

- Cut point: about 1/3 into the movement. Overlap: 2-4 frames at 24 fps.

## Pitfalls

- The hand that reaches with the right hand in A and the left in B: motion hides position errors, not a changed hand (cinewright-continuity).
- Cutting at the end of a move after it has settled loses the cover the motion gave.

## Verify

- In `edit/cut.md`, every cut between shots of one moment is marked `on action` with the motion named, or the reason it is not.
