# Cutting a battle

## Rules

- Geography first: open each phase on a wide that shows where both sides and the hero are (cinewright-movement, entry battle-scenes, plans these cards). Return to a wide every 3-4 fight cards so the audience never loses the map.
- Between wides, cut the small fights: one exchange per shot, attack then reaction, the reaction held longer than the blow.
- Side A always moves one screen direction, side B the other. Never cut two shots where the same side faces opposite ways unless a turn is shown; that reads as a retreat.
- Cross-cut two fronts or the hero and the commander by phase: return to each line at a new state (advancing, held, broken), never to the same state.
- Pace: tighten ASL as a phase climbs, then hold one long shot at the turn (a fall, a banner going down, a silence). Constant fast cutting flattens the battle into noise.
- Scale: the wide army shots are composites (cinewright-finish, entry crowd-multiplication) or implied by sound (cinewright-sound, entry battle-sound). Do not cut to a rendered mass that shows clones or merged figures; cut to a close fight with the mass heard.
- Use the hero's eyeline into off-screen action to imply what was not rendered, then cut to the result.

## Numbers

- A re-establishing wide at least every 4 cards; fight shots 1-3 s once cut; the turn's held shot 3-6 s.

## Pitfalls

- Twenty fast close shots in a row: the audience cannot tell who is winning.
- A wide that changes the side positions from the last wide: re-render or drop it.

## Verify

- `edit/cut.md` marks wides; count no more than 4 fight shots between them. `continuity diff` shows no DIRECTION finding for the cards used.
