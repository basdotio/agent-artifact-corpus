# Opener shapes: good and weak

Shapes, not templates. Each good version works only because of a detail read in that session; copy the
reasoning, never the wording. Names, companies and details are invented for illustration. The same shapes hold
in any language (write natively, `../../linkedin/references/voice.md` "Language notes").

## Cold, with a hook from their post

Situation: an operations lead at a regional logistics firm posted last week that handheld scanners cut pick
errors, but the night shift took longer to train. The user advises warehouses on training.
`pitch_before_reply: off` (no pitch before a reply).

Weak:
> Hi Maria, I came across your profile and was really impressed by your career in logistics. We help companies
> like yours streamline warehouse operations. Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call next week?

Why weak: praise as an opener, a hook that fits anyone, a tell word, an offer before any reply, and a time ask,
which cold messages do worse with (D7, M16).

Good:
> Hi Maria, you wrote that the scanners cut pick errors but the night shift took longer to train. What finally
> worked for them?

Why good: one detail only she could recognise (D3), about 130 characters (D2), a question she can answer from
her own work in a line, nothing about the user.

## Cold, with no hook

Situation: the profile says "Finance Manager" at a mid-size manufacturer and nothing else. No posts.

Weak:
> Hi Tom, I can see you're passionate about finance automation and driving efficiency across your team.

Why weak: the passion is invented. A wrong detail costs more than none.

Better: skip him until there is a hook (a post, a comment, a shared contact). Tell the user a message with no
hook will do worse, and let them choose. If they still want to write:
> Hi Tom, I spend most of my time around month-end close at manufacturers. What is one thing about close you would
> change if you could?

Why this version: says where the user's interest comes from without offering anything (what they sell stays out
while `pitch_before_reply` is `off`; his profile click answers the rest), and it asks for his view, not a fact that sorts him into buyer or not (a yes/no or sizing question is a qualifier).

## After they engaged

Situation: Priya commented on the user's post about hiring a first salesperson: her first rep took two quarters
to close anything.

Weak:
> Thanks so much for your comment! Great point. I'd love to tell you about how we help founders hire sales
> reps.

Why weak: filler thanks, praise, and a pitch at rung 1.

Good:
> Hi Priya, thanks for the point about ramp time under my hiring post. Was the two-quarter ramp down to the
> product being new, or to the territory?

Why good: the thanks is specific (C5) and the message adds something: a follow-up on what she said (C2).

## New connection

Weak:
> Thanks for connecting! I help SaaS companies grow revenue through outbound. Let me know if you ever want to
> chat.

Good:
> Hi Jonas, good to be connected. How is the quarter going on your side?

Why good: first name, nothing about the user, one open question that costs nothing to answer. A question like
"what are your top priorities for next year" asks for work instead.

## Warm network, `pitch_before_reply: on` (pitch allowed)

Situation: Nadia, a former colleague, now runs a group of physiotherapy clinics (profile). The user has launched an
appointment reminder tool; the persona's Offer and story bank say no-shows were a problem they worked on
together. `pitch_before_reply: on`, recipient outside the EU.

Weak:
> Hi Nadia! I hope this message finds you well! It's been a while. As you may know, I've been working on
> something exciting: a reminder platform with SMS, email, smart rescheduling, analytics and integrations.
> Would you have 30 minutes next week for a demo?

Why weak: essay length, a feature list, stacked asks, exclamation marks, a meeting request.

Good:
> Hi Nadia, hope the clinics are doing well. Since the no-show problem we kept running into, I've built a
> reminder tool for physiotherapy clinics, and it's live now: <link from persona>. I'd be glad to hear what you make
> of it.

Why good: one courtesy line (allowed with warm contacts), the offer tied to a shared fact, one link, one soft
ask, no question stacked on the offer. With `pitch_before_reply: off`, the same message stops after the
courtesy line and asks how the clinics are doing; the offer waits for her reply.

## Role change

Weak:
> Congrats on the new role!! Well deserved!

Good:
> Congratulations on the move to head of procurement at Norden Build, Luis. Eight years running site purchasing
> should make the supplier side familiar ground. What are you taking on first?

Why good: the tie-in comes from his earlier roles on the profile; the question is open and answerable in a line.

## Connection note, and none

Situation A: the user and Sara traded two comments yesterday under a post on food costs. No note: the exchange
carries the context, and a free account keeps its few notes for strangers (R19).

Situation B: Sara published a write-up on menu costing for small hotel kitchens; no prior contact.

Weak:
> I'd love to add you to my professional network to explore potential synergies.

Good:
> Hi Sara, I read your write-up on menu costing for small hotel kitchens. I run the same numbers for two
> restaurants and would be glad to connect.

Why good: one line within the note cap (`../../linkedin/references/voice.md`, "Per-surface shape"), a real
reason, no pitch (R16).

## Warm lead from a reaction

Situation: Ben reacted to the user's post about switching payroll providers. Headline: operations manager at a
40-person agency. No comment.

Weak:
> Hi Ben, I saw you liked my post! Are you currently looking for a new payroll provider?

Why weak: reads as watched, and the question is a sales qualifier dressed as interest.

Good:
> Hi Ben, thanks for reacting to the payroll post. Has your team been through a provider switch already, or is it
> still ahead of you?

Why good: says where the contact came from (L3), asks about his side, two answers that each take a line.

## Warm leads in a batch

Two commenters on the same post get openers that share no sentence and no structure. If the second draft starts
to echo the first ("thanks for the point about..."), rebuild it from that person's comment: a batch of
lookalike messages is what untargeted promotion looks like (R16).
