# Limits of the Model

Read this before delivering any verdict. A skill that applies Cagan without knowing
where they are weak produces confident bad advice.

## 1. It assumes autonomy that is often absent

The model presupposes a degree of team autonomy that is rare in large, matricial or
regulated organisations. Applied without executive backing, it produces **frustration
rather than transformation** — a team that knows the standard, cannot reach it, and
now resents the gap.

*When this matters:* diagnose mode, applied to an org the user does not control. Say so
explicitly rather than issuing findings they have no lever on.
*When this does not matter:* the user's own projects, where they are the empowered team.
This is the default assumption for this skill.

## 2. The evidence base is anecdotal

Built on the author's own experience and on examples from the same small set of
reference companies (the usual Silicon Valley names). There is no controlled study
here. Treat the model as a **well-argued craft tradition**, not as an empirical
finding. Do not cite it as though it were evidence.

## 3. It is weak in B2B with contractual commitments

Long sales cycles, systems integrators, contractual feature commitments, procurement
— the model has little to say to these beyond the high-integrity commitment idea,
which is a patch rather than an answer. If the situation is genuinely B2B-contractual,
say the model is thin there instead of forcing it.

## 4. The organisational half is deliberately incomplete

*Inspired* is about the team and the process. The companions are:
- **Empowered** (2020) — leadership and coaching
- **Transformed** (2024) — moving an organisation from the project model to the product model

If a question is really about leadership behaviour or about org transformation, name
the gap and point at the right book rather than over-extending this one.

## 5. Discovery can become its own form of procrastination

Not Cagan's own caveat, but the honest failure mode of applying him: "we need more
evidence" is available forever, and used as a way to never commit. The guard is the
**cheapest-next-move** rule — every blocked gate must name a *specific, cheap, time-boxed*
test, or be closed as an explicit accepted unknown. A gate that stays open with no
named test is the skill failing, not working.

## Honest-verdict rule

If the model does not have a good answer for the situation in front of you, say that.
A verdict of "Cagan is thin here, and here is why" is a legitimate output of this
skill. Forcing the framework onto a case it does not fit is not.
