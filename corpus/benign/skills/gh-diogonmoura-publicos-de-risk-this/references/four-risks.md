# The Four Risks

The spine of the book. Every idea carries four risks, and **all four must be
addressed before you commit to building** — not after.

| Risk | The question | Who owns it |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Value** | Will people want this? Will they buy it / choose it / switch to it? | Product |
| **Usability** | Can they figure out how to use it? | Design |
| **Feasibility** | Can we actually build it, with the people, tech and time we have? | Engineering |
| **Business viability** | Does it work *for us*? Legal, financial, commercial, brand, support cost, sales model. | Product |

## Why this ordering matters

Value first, always. A thing nobody wants is not saved by being usable, buildable
or profitable. If value is blocked, the other three gates are wasted work — mark them
**not reached** and stop.

## What counts as evidence (and what does not)

**Not evidence of value:**
- A stakeholder asked for it — that is a request, not evidence
- A customer asked for it — customers describe solutions, not problems
- A competitor has it — that is evidence *they* built it, nothing more
- Your own conviction, however strong
- A business case built on numbers that were chosen to make the case pass
- "Everyone agrees it's obvious"

**Evidence of value:**
- Someone did something costly: paid, pre-ordered, signed, switched, returned
- A fake door / landing page with real traffic and a measured conversion
- A concierge test where you did it manually and they kept coming back
- Qualitative: users tried a prototype and were visibly disappointed to lose it
- Quantitative: an A/B test on a live surface

**Evidence of business viability** means a *named person* signed off on the legal,
financial, commercial and brand questions. "Someone in legal will probably be fine
with it" is a blocked gate, not a cleared one.

## The uncomfortable statistics

Cagan's claim, and the reason discovery exists at all:

- Most ideas do not produce the result expected of them
- The ones that do usually need **several iterations** to get there

A roadmap of features with dates promises precisely the thing that is not knowable.

## Accepted unknowns

A gate may be closed by **evidence** or by an **explicit accepted unknown** — a
written statement of what you are choosing not to find out, why, and what it costs
if you are wrong. An accepted unknown is a decision. Silence is not.
