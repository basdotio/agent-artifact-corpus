# Roadmaps, Vision and Strategy

## The case against the traditional roadmap

Two facts, and the conclusion follows from them:

1. Most ideas do not produce the outcome expected of them.
2. The ones that do usually need several iterations to get there.

A roadmap of features with dates therefore promises **precisely what is not
knowable**: that this specific solution will work, and that it will work first try,
by this date.

The roadmap is not evil because it plans. It is broken because it commits to
*solutions* when only the *problem* is known.

## What replaces it

**Business context** — so the team can decide well without asking:
- **Product vision.** A three-to-ten-year horizon, in service of the customer,
  inspiring. Deliberately *not* a specification. If it reads like a backlog, it is
  not a vision.
- **Product strategy.** A *sequence* of markets or segments, attacked one at a time.
  The discipline is the sequencing — pleasing everyone simultaneously is the failure
  mode, not the goal.
- **Product principles.** The things you will and will not trade off, decided once,
  so they are not relitigated per decision.

**Objectives** — per team, in OKR form. The team gets the outcome, not the feature.

**High-integrity commitments** — firm dates, reserved for the cases where a date is
genuinely needed (a contract, a trade show, a dependency someone else has planned
around). The point is that they are *rare and honoured*, not that they are abolished.
A team that never commits to a date is as broken as one that only commits to dates.

## Evangelism

Communicating the *why* — continuously, to everyone, past the point where you are
bored of saying it — is part of the job, not an extra. A team that does not know why
cannot make good decisions without you, and then you are the bottleneck.

## Reframing test

When converting a feature roadmap to outcomes, each item must survive this:

- What **outcome** is this feature a guess at? (If none, the feature has no reason
  to exist.)
- What **measure** tells us the outcome moved?
- Is the date a **high-integrity commitment** (something real depends on it) or a
  date someone typed into a slide? Name which.
- If this feature turned out not to move the outcome, **would we notice, and would
  we stop?**

An item that cannot answer question 1 is not a roadmap item. It is somebody's
preference.
