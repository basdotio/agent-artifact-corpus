# Statutes and revised editions

Checked against official AGC guidance on **20 October 2026**. Recheck on each run;
the attached style guides are editorial references, not a current legislation service.

## Current citation and historical identity

[Interpretation Act 1965, s 8](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/IA1965?ProvIds=pr8-)
sets out permissible ways of referring to written laws. Distinguish that legal
sufficiency rule from a court's or publisher's more detailed citation format.

AGC's [Revised Editions of Legislation](https://www.agc.gov.sg/our-roles/drafter-of-laws/revised-editions-of-legislation/)
explains the 2020 Revised Edition, operative from **31 December 2021**. Revised Act
short titles include a year, and chapter numbers are generally unnecessary for
current citations. The same page records completed **2024 and 2025 Revised Editions
of Subsidiary Legislation** and continuing revision work. Check the individual
instrument; neither year is a universal edition for all subsidiary legislation.

| Purpose | Approach |
|---|---|
| Refer to an Act under its modern short title | Use its verified title and year, e.g. `Interpretation Act 1965 s 9A`. Short-title sufficiency does not eliminate house-style requirements. |
| Give an edition-specific citation in SLR style | Where appropriate, use `Interpretation Act 1965 (2020 Rev Ed) s 9A`; separately record the actual version date examined. |
| Cite a pre-2021 historical revised Act | Preserve its verified historical title, chapter and edition, e.g. `Interpretation Act (Cap 1, 2002 Rev Ed)`. Verify the provision in that version. |
| Refer to a repealed or re-enacted statute | Identify the intended enactment and historical edition. A familiar title alone may identify the wrong Act. |
| Cite subsidiary legislation | Read its citation provision, title, revision history and selected edition. Verify the regulation, rule, order, paragraph or schedule as appropriate. |

Do not append `(2020 Rev Ed)` to every statute. Later enactments may not belong to
that edition, and a revised edition of a parent Act does not determine the edition
of regulations made under it. Do not remove the enactment/consolidation year from
a modern title merely because an older precedent lacks it.

## Renumbering and checking older authorities

AGC's [Guide to the 2020 Revised Edition of Acts](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/109/5c9bcf14-3bce-4c78-935e-1030416bc428/guide-to-the-2020-revised-edition-of-acts.pdf),
printed pp xi–xiv, explains title years (including consolidation years), omitted
provisions, and renumbering. Comparative tables map sections and subsections, not
necessarily every lower-level item. The [list of Acts renumbered at section level](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/109/ff5566b7-98b8-4288-aafa-4f04dd1e7958/list-of-acts-renumbered-at-section-level-%285-1-22%29.pdf)
is an initial check, not proof that every other locator is unchanged.

For a provision cited in an older judgment:

1. Retrieve the version that judgment construed. Preserve the old citation and quote
   as historical material, even when supplying a present-day cross-reference.
2. Inspect the comparative table and source notes. Compare the actual old and new
   provision text, including subsections, paragraphs, definitions and schedules.
3. Record both locators and the evidence supporting the mapping. If no authoritative
   mapping is available, state that manual comparison is provisional. Do not map by
   number alone or assume equivalent wording establishes an identical legal effect.
4. Separate a revision or renumbering from a substantive amendment, repeal and
   re-enactment, or commencement event. Assess whether the case's reasoning still
   applies to the operative text; record any unresolved interpretive issue.
5. Inspect subsidiary legislation for references to old parent-Act numbering and
   check whether those references have been updated or need construction. An apparent
   mismatch is a research issue, not a licence to silently correct enacted text.

For SSO's renumbering guidance and publication-status explanations consult its
[FAQ, especially B1 and E1–E3](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/FAQ). Retrieve SSO text under
[access and retrieval conduct](sources-authority.md#access-and-retrieval-conduct): open
the provision view through a permitted route. The skill applies the 3–7 a.m.
Singapore-time condition to automated collection through browsers as well as scripts,
unless applicable AGC permission supports the use. Otherwise try independent sources
and, if necessary, ask the user for the exact manually saved view. Use
`ValidDate=YYYYMMDD` for a past version and record the page's status line. If a source
blocks retrieval, disclose that and use a user-supplied copy or official download rather
than an unverified third-party paraphrase. Follow [statutory-source fallbacks](statute-source-fallbacks.md)
for independent publications, authorised legislation services and version-labelled
repository copies; preserve an unresolved version finding if currency cannot be proved.

## Version receipt

For each decisive statutory proposition record: instrument title and identifier;
Act versus subsidiary legislation; selected law-as-at date; edition; provision;
old/new mapping where relevant; commencement evidence; subsequent amendments and
savings reviewed; source URL and publication authority. Put the result in the source's
`version_review` field and preserve the supporting documents. An edition year is
not a current-through date: later amendments must still be checked.

For saved research, apply [statutory evidence](statutory-evidence.md): retain the exact
cited provision and parent context, with a structured identity/version receipt for
the current run. A fresh or revised draft needs renewed instrument identification,
version selection and provision-specific checking, tied to that draft snapshot.
Resolve the draft's own abbreviations; do not carry mappings across matters. Check
that the intended principal Act, predecessor/re-enactment, amendment or subsidiary
instrument is actually the one cited, rather than merely finding the same section
number in a similarly named instrument.

SSO is an AGC research service; distinguish a web consolidation from the authoritative
published legislation and revised edition. Verify the source's publication status
and use the appropriate Gazette/revised text where authenticity matters. A website
timestamp, PDF download date, or the original Act's commencement date alone does not
establish the version governing the client's events.
