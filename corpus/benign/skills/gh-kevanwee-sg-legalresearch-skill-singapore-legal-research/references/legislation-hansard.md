# Legislation, versions, and Hansard

Begin with the enacted provision, not an account of what a Bill was intended to do.
The primary starting point for extrinsic materials is [Interpretation Act 1965,
s 9A](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/IA1965?ProvIds=pr9A-). Read the selected version in
full, including subsections (2)–(4), before asserting that a particular speech can
resolve a question. This reference is a research method, not a substitute for it.

## Build a provision history

1. Identify the current and relevant historical provision in SSO; capture the selected
   date and displayed status. Check the Act's definitions and subsidiary legislation.
2. Use its legislative history and amendment notes to identify the Act that inserted
   or changed the relevant words. Trace renumbering and consequential amendments.
3. Locate that amending Act and its commencement instrument in official sources.
   Verify the exact section's operative date and any savings, transitional provisions,
   deferred commencement, or prospective amendments. A revised edition is not itself
   proof of a substantive amendment.
4. Match the enacting or amending Act to the correct Bill number, year, explanatory
   statement and clause. Do not confuse Bill clauses with enacted section numbers.
5. Search Parliament's [Official Reports](https://www.parliament.gov.sg/parliamentary-business/official-reports-(parl-debates))
   and [SPRS](https://sprs.parl.gov.sg/search/) for the Bill's Second Reading debate,
   ministerial introduction and reply, and relevant exchanges or committee materials.
   Search former titles, dates, subject terms, and clause numbers if necessary.
6. Read the whole relevant exchange. Attribute each statement to its speaker and role.
   Distinguish a moving Minister's explanation from an MP's question, a consultation,
   an answer on a different Bill, and later policy commentary. Record whether a
   ministerial reply accepted or rejected the premise of the question.

## Analyse purpose without replacing the text

Explain the available textual meanings, the purpose supported by the enactment and
relevant materials, and which interpretation promotes that purpose. Explain the
statutory basis for considering the particular extrinsic material and the weight
given to it. Do not impose a universal “ambiguity first” gate on all uses of s 9A,
or treat a speech as amending the enacted provision.

An important research lead is [*Tan Cheng Bock v Attorney-General*, [2017] SGCA
50](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2017_SGCA_50), especially its statutory
interpretation discussion. Read and check its subsequent treatment for the actual
question before relying on it. A link in this skill is not a current-law certification.

## Report what can be established

Deliver a timeline with columns for instrument, provision or clause, event, date,
source link, and significance. Keep enactment, publication, commencement, and
revision separate. Append relevant passages with accurate locators and speaker
attribution. For older debates preserve the historical legislature and title.

Do not invent volume or column numbers from a print view's browser page count.
When the database gives no conventional locator, retain its topic identifier,
speech title, date, speaker, stable link and available page/paragraph locator,
then apply the SLR guide's electronic citation format and disclose the limitation.
If the site needs JavaScript or denies automated requests, use an available authorised
browser or a supplied export. Report the access gap if neither is possible. Never
equate a failed fetch with the absence of parliamentary materials.
