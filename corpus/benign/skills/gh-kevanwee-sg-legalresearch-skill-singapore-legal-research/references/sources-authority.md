# Singapore sources and authority

Source map checked on **20 October 2026**. Refresh links and operative instruments at
each research run. A retrieval platform is not a separate tier of legal authority.

## Authority and reasoning

- **Constitution:** start with [Article 4](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CONS1963?ProvIds=pr4-)
  for constitutional supremacy. Constitutional questions require their own interpretive
  authorities; a general source diagram does not resolve validity.
- **Acts and subsidiary legislation:** use [Singapore Statutes Online (SSO)](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/).
  Subsidiary legislation depends on its enabling legislation. Check the empowering
  provision, commencement, scope, and relevant challenges to validity. Read schedules.
- **Judicial decisions:** read the court's reasons. Court level, the point actually
  decided, subsequent treatment, and statutory change determine weight. The report
  publisher, availability online, and publication year alone do not.
- **Common law and equity:** consider their interaction with Singapore legislation and
  local precedent. For reception questions examine the [Application of English Law Act
  1993](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/AELA1993), particularly ss 3–5; do not import modern
  English decisions or statutes automatically.
- **Special regimes:** identify the relevant legislation and appellate structure for
  family, Syariah, tribunal, regulatory, disciplinary, or international commercial work.
  Verify any asserted custom rather than treating practice as law.
- **Treaties and soft law:** establish domestic implementation and the asserted legal
  effect. A treaty, consultation, regulator's guideline, and binding statutory notice
  are different instruments even when hosted by the same agency.

## Courts and precedent

The [Judiciary's account of Supreme Court roles](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/who-we-are/role-structure-supreme-court)
and the [Supreme Court of Judicature Act 1969](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/SCJA1969)
are starting points for jurisdiction. The Court of Appeal is the final appellate
court. The High Court comprises the Appellate Division and General Division; the
SICC is a division of the General Division. Civil appeal allocation is statutory;
the Appellate Division is not a mandatory intermediate step in every appeal.

For precedent, prioritise applicable Court of Appeal holdings, then relevant local
appellate and first-instance decisions. Resolve conflicts by examining the actual
rules of precedent and treatment rather than choosing the newest judgment. Do not
claim that coordinate first-instance judges always bind one another. An appellate
outcome does not necessarily endorse every part of the reasons below. Older Privy
Council and historical local decisions require their own status check.

[Supreme Court Practice Directions 2021, paragraph 106](https://epd2021-supremecourt.judiciary.gov.sg/part-11-conduct-of-court-proceedings#id-106-citation-of-judgments)
addresses proposition-specific use of authorities, preference for directly relevant
local decisions, report priority, and adverse authority. For court work, check the
[applicable Practice Directions](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/news-and-resources/practice-directions/supreme-court-practice-directions):
the 2013 and 2021 regimes can apply to different proceedings. Other courts and the
SICC have their own directions. A publishing style guide does not replace them.

citations, party names, and legal synonyms; refine to official domains. A plausible
eLitigation URL is only a candidate until opened and matched to the decision.

## Browser discovery

Establish live access before starting live legal research. Tool availability varies
by host; discover the capabilities actually exposed in the current session rather
than assuming that a named browser integration is installed.

1. **Discover and load.** Inspect the exposed tool/skill catalog, including deferred
   tools discoverable through the host's supported search. Load the instructions for
   the relevant browser integration before using it. Prefer a purpose-built browser
   tool and honour a browser or tab the user specified.
2. **Inspect the route.** Use its documented inventory, attachment or new-tab entry
   point. An empty tab list, empty browser inventory or failed attachment describes
   that route only. Where supported, open a new tab or launch an installed browser
   through the documented entry point; an existing attached tab is not a prerequisite.
3. **Check the alternatives.** If that route is unavailable or technically fails,
   inspect other exposed browser integrations. On Windows, if a computer-use skill
   is available, read it and its required runtime guidance, initialise its supported
   runtime, list installed apps/windows, and select a returned browser window. If
   necessary and supported, launch an installed browser and inspect the new inventory.
   Do not infer that Windows computer use is unavailable from a separate browser
   tool's empty inventory. Use only documented APIs; this skill neither bundles nor
   requires a particular browser package, extension or runtime.
4. **Confirm usable access.** Open a needed official source at reading pace and
   inspect its URL, title and readable source content. A listed browser, successful
   runtime import, search snippet or blank tab does not prove live retrieval. For
   SSO, confirm the instrument, requested provision and displayed version/status;
   save the page privately through the supported browser route before converting it.
   A readable page without a supported save/export route may support transcript-based
   research, but does not establish that the saved-page converter has been live-tested.
5. **Record the outcome.** For saved runs, record the tool/route, retrieval time,
   URL, result and evidence file in private run notes; put the selected access route
   and material limits in the source's `review_note`. Distinguish `not exposed`,
   `empty inventory`, `initialisation/attachment failed`, `tool-enforced stop`,
   `site refused access` and `live retrieval confirmed`. Declare browser access
   unavailable only after checking the exposed alternatives, or explain the explicit
   stop that prevented further discovery. Never label offline fixtures as live tests.

Discovery fallbacks address missing capabilities and technical failures; they are
not a way around restrictions. Follow each tool's documented recovery limits. A
tool-enforced instruction to stop the turn ends the attempt immediately; do not
switch runtimes to evade it. A site block or challenge ends requests to that site,
including through another browser, session or network route. If no permitted browser
route works, use supplied/saved material or the permitted scripted-request route
below. For legislation, also try [independent statutory sources](statute-source-fallbacks.md)
through available authorised tools; SSO unavailability does not end statutory research.
Disclose what remains unverified. Do not install software or extensions,
change security settings, or request credentials merely to complete discovery.

## Access and retrieval conduct

Reviewed on **20 October 2026** against the [SSO Terms of Use](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Terms-of-Use),
[SSO FAQ A3](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/FAQ), [SSO robots.txt](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/robots.txt) and the
[eLitigation Terms of Use](https://www.elitigation.sg/_layouts/IELS/Home/eLitigation_Terms_of_Use.htm).
The latest terms/FAQ review used search-indexed publisher text; direct web-reader
opens returned HTTP 403. That review does not establish live provision access.
Recheck them when they matter. Use these retrieval routes, in order, falling back only
when the one above is unavailable or fails. Complete [browser discovery](#browser-discovery)
before concluding that route 1 is unavailable:

These are access routes to a publisher, not a requirement to depend on SSO. If SSO
is unavailable or refuses access, use the [statutory-source fallback workflow](statute-source-fallbacks.md)
to retrieve from an independent publisher, with that publisher's own terms and a
separate version check. Do not retry SSO through an alternative client after a block.

1. **An agent-operated real browser.** Use a browser the user has made available, such
   as Claude in Chrome, the desktop app's browser, or a locally installed browser under
   automation, to open and save the page as a reader would:
   - Use the real browser as it is. Do not add stealth plugins, hide that it is
     automated, change its fingerprint or user-agent, or solve CAPTCHAs.
   - Open only the pages the question needs, at human pace (about ten seconds or more
     between page views), and save each page's HTML (a screenshot helps review).
   - Successful loading establishes technical access, not permission for every
     automated use. Apply the publisher's conditions to what the agent actually
     does, including collecting text or saving pages. If a CAPTCHA, block page or
     other challenge appears, stop and fall back; do not retry or answer it.
   - Earlier Chrome success is environment-specific evidence, not a guarantee of
     current availability or an exemption from the publisher's conditions.
2. **Material the user supplies or saves in a normal browser.** For legislation, ask for
   the SSO provision view (e.g. `/Act/CA2021?ProvIds=pr146-`) saved as HTML or printed
   to PDF. List the exact links so the user can save them in one pass.
3. **Scripted requests**, only within the site's terms as set out below.

**SSO: browsing versus extraction.** Clause 13(d), also described in FAQ A3(d),
conditions automated extraction for use/reproduction on **3 a.m. to 7 a.m. Singapore
time** and non-disruptive, non-deceptive conduct. It does not state a bulk-only
threshold or an agent-browser exemption. This is not a general opening-hours rule
for a person reading SSO manually. However, collecting provision text or saving
pages by an agent can be automated extraction even through a real browser.

**Maintainer policy (adopted 20 October 2026).** The skill's maintainer has decided
how this skill treats the two routes. This is the maintainer's reading of the terms,
not AGC's classification, and it does not establish permission:

- **Agent-operated real-browser reading is allowed at any hour.** For the user's
  current research, an agent may open in a real browser the provision views, and the
  single Act or version download, that the question actually needs, at reading pace
  (about ten seconds or more between page views), keeping copies only as private
  research evidence. The maintainer treats this as reading for the user's own research,
  not the extraction for use or reproduction that the window conditions. Do not browse
  more of the statute book than the question needs, and never redistribute or publish
  what was read.
- **Scripted or bulk collection stays within the window.** Script requests, whole-Act
  harvesting beyond the question, corpus building and repeated collection remain limited
  to 3 a.m. to 7 a.m. Singapore time, with honest identification. A scripted client that
  SSO refuses is refused: never imitate a browser to get past it.

Record the route in each source's `review_note` (agent-operated real browser, time
retrieved) so a reviewer can see which policy applied. Do not infer an exemption from
slow requests, a small page count, or a page loading successfully, and record any AGC
permission and its scope if one is obtained. Without a working real browser outside the
window, continue immediately with independent statutory sources or a copy the user has
already saved; there is no need to suspend research. If those cannot establish the needed
version, give the user the exact manual-save link and explain the remaining check. Do not
contact AGC without user instructions.

Within a permitted automated session, fetch only the views needed, once, at least
six seconds apart (the previously observed robots crawl delay); recheck current
robots instructions when accessible. A site refusal ends the attempt regardless
of time. Time-window compliance alone never establishes access or permission.

For a past version, add `ValidDate=YYYYMMDD` to the provision view, e.g.
`/Act/CA2021?ProvIds=pr146-&ValidDate=20220101`. Record the page's status line (such as
"Not current version (effective from 31 Dec 2021 to 31 Mar 2022)" or "Current version
as at …") as the source version, and check the version list for amending Acts. The
bundled `extract-sso` command reports both from a saved page; see
[statutory evidence](statutory-evidence.md).

**eLitigation.** Its terms limit use to authorised purposes and bar reproduction
without approval; they give no permission for automated access, and there is no
`robots.txt`. Script retrieval conservatively, if at all: a handful of judgments, spaced
requests, private copies only.

For every site:

- Identify the client honestly. Never spoof a browser user-agent or fingerprint.
- Never solve, bypass or evade a challenge, CAPTCHA, firewall, rate limit, login or
  paywall, and never rotate addresses or proxies to avoid one.
- On a block or challenge (for example an empty HTTP 202, 403, 429 or CAPTCHA page),
  stop requesting from that site. Do not retry in a loop. Record the access gap and
  move to the next route.
- Reuse copies already saved in the run rather than fetching the same page again.
- Record the access route in the source's `review_note`: user-saved browser copy,
  agent-operated browser, or scripted request with its time and user-agent.
- Keep saved copies as private research evidence. Reproducing SSO legislation in a
  published work is subject to clause 13's conditions; do not publish downloads.

## Treatment record

For every decisive case, record the last search date, sources queried, queries and
date coverage, later decisions actually read, appeal status, and statutory changes.
Use a precise conclusion: “No adverse treatment found in the listed public searches
through [date]; subscription citator not checked.” If adverse treatment is found,
state the later court, paragraph, and exact proposition affected. Do not use a
whole-case label where only one proposition was limited.
