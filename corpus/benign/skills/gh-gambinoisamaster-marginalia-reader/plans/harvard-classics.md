# The Harvard Classics — Fifteen Minutes a Day

A daily reading plan from *Fifteen Minutes a Day: The Reading Guide* to the Harvard Classics, the 50-volume "five-foot shelf" edited by Charles W. Eliot. Every day of the year pairs a short reading (about fifteen minutes) with an anniversary and a teaser. The guide is in the public domain; the hooks below are paraphrased.

Volume and page numbers refer to the original P. F. Collier volumes. Scans of the whole set, and of the guide itself, are free on the Internet Archive. In another edition, find the passage by its content.

Go at your own pace and track your place by entry, not by calendar date. Starting at January 1 in October is a perfectly good way to do it.

**About the notes.** Each Note is the guide's own anniversary line, transcribed as originally printed. A few of its dates and spellings differ from modern references.

**Corrections welcome.** All 366 days (including Feb 29) were transcribed from a scan of the guide, with every volume and page number checked against the page images. If something doesn't match your copy, please open an issue or a pull request.

## January

### Jan 1 · Franklin's Advice for the New Year
Franklin, *Autobiography* — Vol. 1, pp. 79–85
Hook: Franklin's virtue of Resolution: resolve to do what you ought, then do what you resolved.

### Jan 2 · School-Day Poems of John Milton
Milton, *Poems* — Vol. 4, pp. 7–18
Hook: Verses Milton wrote as a schoolboy, already hinting at what was coming.
Note: First edition of Milton's collected poems published Jan. 2, 1645

### Jan 3 · Cicero on Friendship
Cicero, *On Friendship* — Vol. 9, pp. 16–26
Hook: Rome's great orator ranks friendship as useful as fire and water.
Note: Cicero born Jan. 3, 106 B.C.

### Jan 4 · A Flounder Fish Story
Grimm, *Fairy Tales* — Vol. 17, pp. 83–90
Hook: A fisherman frees a talking flounder, and his wife keeps demanding more miracles.
Note: Jacob Grimm, elder of the famous Grimm brothers, born Jan. 4, 1785

### Jan 5 · The Soaring Eagle and Contented Stork
Mazzini, *Byron and Goethe* — Vol. 32, pp. 377–396
Hook: The exiled Italian patriot compares Byron to a soaring eagle and Goethe to a contented stork.
Note: Byron arrived in Greece to fight for Greek freedom, Jan. 5, 1824

### Jan 6 · Warned by Hector's Ghost
Virgil, *Aeneid* — Vol. 13, pp. 109–127
Hook: Hector's ghost warns Aeneas to flee doomed Troy, carrying his father and leading his son.
Note: H. Schliemann, discoverer of ancient Troy, born Jan. 6, 1822

### Jan 7 · If He Yawned, She Lost Her Head!
*The Thousand and One Nights* — Vol. 16, pp. 5–13
Hook: A sultan executes each new bride at dawn, until Scheherazade starts telling stories.

### Jan 8 · Trying the Patience of Job
*The Book of Job* — Vol. 44, pp. 71–87
Hook: Satan claims Job is pious only because he prospers, and the trial begins.

### Jan 9 · A Treasure Hunt in Nombre de Dios
Nichols, *Sir Francis Drake Revived* — Vol. 33, pp. 135–145
Hook: Drake and fifty-two men go after Spain's treasure at Nombre de Dios.
Note: Drake died at Nombre de Dios, Jan. 9, 1596

### Jan 10 · Where Love Lies Waiting
Euripides, *The Bacchae* — Vol. 8, pp. 368–372
Hook: King Pentheus of Thebes sets himself against the god Dionysus, who has bewitched the city's women.

### Jan 11 · Hamilton—Father of Wall Street
*The Federalist* — Vol. 43, pp. 199–207
Hook: Hamilton, the Treasury, and the papers that helped bring New York into the Union.
Note: Alexander Hamilton born Jan. 11, 1757

### Jan 12 · What Is Good Taste?
Burke, *On Taste* — Vol. 24, pp. 11–26
Hook: A sultan spots one gruesome flaw in a painting of John the Baptist's severed head. Good judge of art, or not?
Note: Edmund Burke born Jan. 12, 1729

### Jan 13 · Rousseau Seeks Sanctuary in England
Rousseau, *Inquiry on Inequality* — Vol. 34, pp. 215–228
Hook: Rousseau traces human inequality back to our primitive beginnings, ideas that drove him into exile.
Note: Jean Jacques Rousseau arrived in England, Jan. 13, 1766

### Jan 14 · The First Step Toward Independence
*The Fundamental Orders of Connecticut* — Vol. 43, pp. 60–65
Hook: Billed as the first written constitution that limits government's power.
Note: Fundamental Orders of Connecticut adopted Jan. 14, 1639

### Jan 15 · "The Moving Finger Writes"
*The Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam* — Vol. 41, pp. 943–953
Hook: A philosophy of enjoying life's good things, the classic book to dip into.
Note: "Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam" first published Jan. 15, 1859

### Jan 16 · The Old Woman and the Wine Jar
*Aesop's Fables* — Vol. 17, pp. 43–44; also pp. 31–43
Hook: An old woman sniffs an empty wine jar, and remembers.

### Jan 17 · Franklin's Family Tree
Franklin, *Autobiography* — Vol. 1, pp. 5–15
Hook: The youngest son of the youngest son, for five generations.
Note: Benjamin Franklin born Jan. 17, 1706

### Jan 18 · Origin of Yale "Brekekekex-Ko-ax"
Aristophanes, *The Frogs* — Vol. 8, pp. 439–449
Hook: Dionysus and his slave Xanthias open with a comic double act, and a chorus of frogs gave Yale a cheer.

### Jan 19 · Poe on Poetry
Poe, *The Poetic Principle* — Vol. 28, pp. 371–380
Hook: Poe takes apart what poetry is made of.
Note: Edgar Allan Poe born Jan. 19, 1809

### Jan 20 · "Ah! It Is St. Agnes' Eve—"
Keats, *Eve of St. Agnes* — Vol. 41, pp. 883–893
Hook: On St. Agnes' Eve, maidens are promised visions of their lovers, and Porphyro makes use of the custom.
Note: St. Agnes' Eve, Jan. 20

### Jan 21 · The Nightingale's Healing Melody
Andersen, *Tales* — Vol. 17, pp. 301–310
Hook: A dying emperor, Death at the bedside, and a bird's song.

### Jan 22 · A King's Pleasure Now Yours
Corneille, *Polyeucte* — Vol. 26, pp. 77–87
Hook: French classical drama, written to please kings.
Note: Corneille elected to French Academy, Jan. 22, 1647

### Jan 23 · Pascal Knew Men and Triangles
Pascal, *The Art of Persuasion* — Vol. 48, pp. 400–411
Hook: A mathematician measures human conceit as precisely as a triangle.
Note: Pascal publishes "Provincial Letters," Jan. 23, 1656

### Jan 24 · Odysseus Silenced the Sirens
Homer, *Odyssey* — Vol. 22, pp. 165–173
Hook: Wax in the crew's ears, and Odysseus bound to the mast so he can listen.

### Jan 25 · A Field Mouse Made Famous
Burns, "To a Mouse" and other poems — Vol. 6, pp. 119–120, 388–394
Hook: A ploughman apologizes to the mouse whose nest he turned up.
Note: Robert Burns born Jan. 25, 1759

### Jan 26 · In the Cradle of Civilization
Herodotus, *An Account of Egypt* — Vol. 33, pp. 65–75
Hook: Old Egypt as gossip: sacred bulls and cats, court scandal, a daughter entombed in a golden cow.

### Jan 27 · Dante and Beatrice in Paradise
Dante, *Divine Comedy* — Vol. 20, pp. 267–279
Hook: The woman he loved at first sight, and may never have spoken to, meets him in Paradise.
Note: Dante victim of political persecution in Florence, Jan. 27, 1302

### Jan 28 · Man's Wings
Thomas à Kempis — Vol. 7, pp. 242–249
Hook: Simplicity and purity as the wings that lift us above earthly things.

### Jan 29 · Visits the Land of Fire
Darwin, *Voyage of the Beagle* — Vol. 29, pp. 209–221
Hook: Darwin's study of Tierra del Fuego and its people.
Note: Darwin married Emma Wedgewood, Jan. 29, 1839

### Jan 30 · First Problem Play Popular
Sophocles, *Antigone* — Vol. 8, pp. 255–266
Hook: Antigone defies a king's decree out of duty to her brother. What duty?
Note: Sophocles died at Athens, Jan. 30, 405 B.C.

### Jan 31 · What "Don Quixote" Really Slew
*Don Quixote* — Vol. 14, pp. 60–67
Hook: An amateur knight sets out with windmills, damsels, and a great deal of satire ahead of him.

## February

### Feb 1 · King Arthur's Knights Find Holy Grail
Malory, *The Holy Grail* — Vol. 35, pp. 112–123
Hook: A crack of thunder shakes the castle, and the Round Table knights see the Holy Grail appear, draped in white.

### Feb 2 · "Apparel Oft Proclaims the Man"
Shakespeare, *Hamlet* — Vol. 46, pp. 107–120
Hook: Polonius sends Laertes abroad with advice on manners and clothes, while Prince Hamlet has to learn the hard way.
Note: Shakespeare's twins—Hamnet and Judith—baptized Feb. 2, 1585

### Feb 3 · A House of Mirth and Revelry
Jonson, *The Alchemist* — Vol. 47, pp. 543–558
Hook: The master is away and his servant is minding the house: cue boisterous, absurd goings-on.
Note: Ben Jonson receives life pension from James I, Feb. 3, 1619

### Feb 4 · "Genius, a Secret to Itself"
Carlyle, *Characteristics* — Vol. 25, pp. 319–327
Hook: Carlyle argues that the greatest thinkers never sense their own power, and that every mind has its own character.
Note: Thomas Carlyle died Feb. 4, 1881

### Feb 5 · Diamonds, Diamonds Everywhere!
*The Thousand and One Nights* — Vol. 16, pp. 243–250
Hook: Stranded in a valley strewn with giant diamonds and guarded by deadly snakes, Sindbad needs a clever way out.

### Feb 6 · Charles Lamb Suggests To-day's Reading
Marlowe, *Edward the Second* — Vol. 46, pp. 73–89
Hook: Charles Lamb ranks Marlowe's portrait of a king forced to give up his crown nearly level with Shakespeare's Richard II.
Note: Christopher Marlowe born Feb. 6, 1564

### Feb 7 · A Letter from a Lion
*Letter to Lord Chesterfield* — Vol. 39, pp. 206–207
Hook: Treat Samuel Johnson well and he returns the courtesy; slight him, and you get this letter.
Note: Samuel Johnson writes to Lord Chesterfield, Feb. 7, 1755

### Feb 8 · Tragic Death of a World-Famous Beauty
Burns, *Poems* — Vol. 6, pp. 396–406
Hook: Burns gives voice to Mary, Queen of Scots, imprisoned and longing for the peace of the grave.
Note: Mary, Queen of Scots, beheaded Feb. 8, 1587

### Feb 9 · Rest Between Wars
Tacitus, *On Germany* — Vol. 33, pp. 93–102
Hook: Tacitus visits the Germanic tribes of the Rhine and finds fierce warriors who loaf whenever there's no fighting.

### Feb 10 · No Fancy for a Plain Gentleman
Voltaire, *Letters on the English* — Vol. 34, pp. 130–140
Hook: Congreve asks Voltaire to see him as a mere gentleman, not a playwright, and gets a sharp reply.
Note: William Congreve baptized Feb. 10, 1670

### Feb 11 · The Queen Freezes Her Philosophy
Descartes, *Discourse on Method* — Vol. 34, pp. 5–20
Hook: A queen's demand for lessons at icy dawn cost Descartes his life, but his method lives on in this essay.
Note: René Descartes died at Stockholm, Feb. 11, 1650

### Feb 12 · Oxford Corrects Lincoln's Mistake
Lincoln, *Writings* — Vol. 43, pp. 415–420
Hook: Lincoln thought his Gettysburg Address had flopped; Oxford's Balliol College later hung it in bronze as model English prose.
Note: Abraham Lincoln born Feb. 12, 1809

### Feb 13 · The Frank Story of an Amazing Life
Cellini, *Autobiography* — Vol. 31, pp. 68–80
Hook: Cellini's own account of a Renaissance life full of art, violence, passion, and black magic.
Note: Benvenuto Cellini died Feb. 13, 1570

### Feb 14 · Love Always Young
Pascal, *Discourse on the Passion of Love* — Vol. 48, pp. 411–421
Hook: Pascal, mathematician and moralist, turns to love, and shows a surprisingly warm, human side.
Note: St. Valentine's Day

### Feb 15 · The World Well Lost?
Dryden, *All for Love* — Vol. 18, pp. 53–69
Hook: Dryden's Antony risks command of Rome and his good name for Cleopatra. Was the world well lost?
Note: Mark Antony offers Caesar crown at Rome, Feb. 15, 44 B.C.

### Feb 16 · Social Circles Among Ants
Darwin, *Origin of Species* — Vol. 11, pp. 264–268
Hook: Darwin on slave-making ants, whose captives build the nests, feed their masters, and carry the colony when it moves.

### Feb 17 · Death His Curtain Call
Molière, *Tartuffe* — Vol. 26, pp. 199–217
Hook: Molière, who collapsed on stage in his own play, created Tartuffe, a character to rank with Falstaff.
Note: Molière died Feb. 17, 1673

### Feb 18 · Lasting Peace with Great Britain
*Treaty with Great Britain (1814)* — Vol. 43, pp. 255–264
Hook: The treaty that ended the War of 1812 and began more than a century of unbroken peace with Britain.
Note: Treaty with Great Britain proclaimed Feb. 18, 1815

### Feb 19 · Earthly Experience of a Chinese Goddess
*Buddhist Writings* — Vol. 45, pp. 693–701
Hook: One of the Garland God's heavenly wives tumbles to earth and lives as a mortal temple servant.

### Feb 20 · Voltaire Observes the Quakers
Voltaire, *Letters on the English* — Vol. 34, pp. 65–78
Hook: On his English visit, Voltaire studies the Quakers, named for the way they trembled when the spirit moved them.

### Feb 21 · Does Football Make a College?
Newman, *The Idea of a University* — Vol. 28, pp. 31–39
Hook: Newman asks what truly makes a university: its buildings, its scholars, its students, or its football team?
Note: Cardinal Newman born Feb. 21, 1801

### Feb 22 · An Ode for Washington's Birthday
Burns, *Poems* — Vol. 6, pp. 492–494
Hook: Burns takes up America's harp to praise liberty, and mourns that Britain has turned against it.
Note: George Washington born Feb. 22, 1732

### Feb 23 · Pepys' Nose for News
Stevenson, *Samuel Pepys* — Vol. 28, pp. 285–292
Hook: Stevenson profiles Samuel Pepys, the curious, gossiping diarist who heard every piece of news in court and town.
Note: Samuel Pepys born Feb. 23, 1632

### Feb 24 · Lights and Shadows of Milton
Milton, *Poems* — Vol. 4, pp. 30–38
Hook: Milton banishes gloomy Melancholy and summons the pleasures of youth, pledging himself to them for good.
Note: John Milton marries his third wife, Elizabeth Marshall, Feb. 24, 1662

### Feb 25 · Punished for Too Sharp a Wit
*The Shortest-Way with Dissenters* — Vol. 27, pp. 133–147
Hook: Defoe's satire cut so deep that it earned him a fine, a prison cell, and the pillory.
Note: "The Shortest-Way with Dissenters" censored, Feb. 25, 1703

### Feb 26 · A David Who Side-stepped Goliath
Hugo, *Preface to Cromwell* — Vol. 39, pp. 337–349
Hook: Scorned by France's leading critics, Hugo wrote a preface that armed the rebels against classical rules.
Note: Victor Hugo born Feb. 26, 1802

### Feb 27 · Poet Apostle of Good Cheer
Longfellow, *Poems* — Vol. 42, pp. 1264–1280
Hook: Longfellow's familiar, consoling verses, the ones that have cheered generations of readers.
Note: Longfellow born Feb. 27, 1807

### Feb 28 · Spoke Latin First
Montaigne, *Essays* — Vol. 32, pp. 29–40
Hook: Raised to speak Latin before French, Montaigne spent a lifetime gathering thoughts worth sharing.
Note: Michel de Montaigne born Feb. 28, 1533

### Feb 29 · Goethe's Tale of a Maiden in Love
*Hermann and Dorothea* — Vol. 19, p. 337; also pp. 395–410
Hook: By old tradition, leap day lets a woman propose; Goethe's narrative poem offers a love story that goes smoothly.

## March

### Mar 1 · Invented Sir Roger de Coverly
*The Spectator Club* — Vol. 27, pp. 83–87
Hook: Steele sketches the members of the Spectator's club, among them Sir Roger de Coverly, more memorable than most real people.
Note: First issue of the "Spectator," published March 1, 1711

### Mar 2 · What Sailors Do on Sunday
Dana, *Two Years Before the Mast* — Vol. 23, pp. 112–119
Hook: Dana describes a sailor's single day ashore: dressed in his best, answerable to no one, making the most of it.

### Mar 3 · For Poets and Fishermen
Walton, *Life of George Herbert* — Vol. 15, pp. 373–382
Hook: Izaak Walton, beloved of anglers, tells the life of George Herbert, a country parson whose pastime was poetry.
Note: George Herbert died March 3, 1633

### Mar 4 · Penn—Pioneer, Thinker, and Builder
Penn, *Some Fruits of Solitude* — Vol. 1, pp. 321–330
Hook: Penn, a stubborn Quaker, kept his hat on before the king; these maxims reveal the shrewd mind beneath it.
Note: King Charles grants Penn charter of Pennsylvania, March 4, 1681

### Mar 5 · Laughed at Locks
Cellini, *Autobiography* — Vol. 31, pp. 214–224
Hook: Cellini tells his jailers to guard him well, because he fully intends to escape.

### Mar 6 · West Point's Outcast, America's First Great Poet
Poe, *The Raven* — Vol. 42, pp. 1227–1230
Hook: Poe was expelled, disinherited, and too poor to heat his dying wife's room; "The Raven" mirrors that despair.
Note: Poe expelled from West Point, March 6, 1831

### Mar 7 · Bacon Warns Judges
Bacon, *Of Judicature* — Vol. 3, pp. 130–134
Hook: Bacon reminds judges that their job is to interpret the law, not to write it, in one dense essay.
Note: Bacon made Keeper of the Great Seal of England, March 7, 1616

### Mar 8 · Dangerous Experiment with a Wife
Cervantes, *Don Quixote* — Vol. 14, pp. 307–319
Hook: Convinced his wife is perfect, Anselmo wants proof, and makes a strange request of his closest friend, Lothario.

### Mar 9 · Common Sense and Good Manners
*Treatise on Good Manners* — Vol. 27, pp. 99–103
Hook: Swift, who joked that dueling usefully thinned out the fools, argues that good manners rest on plain common sense.
Note: Passage of laws against dueling in England, March 9, 1679

### Mar 10 · Beaumont—The Adonis of Elizabethan Playwrights
*Philaster* — Vol. 47, pp. 667–677
Hook: Two well-born gentlemen, Beaumont and Fletcher, risked their social standing to write plays; this one made their name.

### Mar 11 · Gain Gleaned from Suffering
Emerson, *Compensation* — Vol. 5, pp. 85–92
Hook: Emerson argues that life keeps a balance sheet: every pain is repaid, and every pleasure has its price.
Note: Emerson ordained Unitarian minister, March 11, 1829

### Mar 12 · An Irish Bishop's Wit
Berkeley, *Three Dialogues* — Vol. 37, pp. 228–238
Hook: Berkeley, a bishop with great hopes for America and plans for a Bermuda college, argues his philosophy in dialogue.
Note: Bishop Berkeley born March 12, 1685

### Mar 13 · Before Nobility Ran Tea Rooms
Manzoni, *I Promessi Sposi* — Vol. 21, pp. 318–332
Hook: Manzoni recreates the swagger and luxury of seventeenth-century Italian nobles, lords of great castles and vast estates.

### Mar 14 · A Maiden's Forfeit
*The Holy Grail* — Vol. 35, pp. 194–200
Hook: Knights at a castle ask whether a traveling gentlewoman is a maiden. What custom will she have to honor?
Note: Malory, recorder of King Arthur stories, died March 14, 1470

### Mar 15 · Beware the Ides of March!
Plutarch, *Caesar* — Vol. 12, pp. 315–321
Hook: Warned twice, and surrounded by dreadful omens, Caesar still walks to the Senate on the Ides of March.
Note: Ides of March, March 15

### Mar 16 · Crabs Climb Trees?
Darwin, *Voyage of the Beagle* — Vol. 29, pp. 466–475
Hook: Darwin describes a tree-climbing crab that comes down from time to time for a dip.

### Mar 17 · An Old Irish Legend
*The Poetry of the Celtic Races* — Vol. 32, pp. 174–182
Hook: When St. Patrick's listeners demand to see Heaven and Hell for themselves, the saint takes them at their word.
Note: St. Patrick's Day

### Mar 18 · New Way to Pay Old Debts
*A New Way to Pay Old Debts* — Vol. 47, pp. 859–870
Hook: Cheated of his inheritance by a crafty uncle and jeered by his former servants, a wastrel plots his comeback.
Note: Massinger buried March 18, 1640

### Mar 19 · Seeing Old Egypt
Herodotus, *An Account of Egypt* — Vol. 33, pp. 72–84
Hook: Herodotus tours ancient Egypt: its strange temples, floating islands, and colossal pyramids.
Note: Last recorded event in Herodotus' history dated March 19, 478 B.C.

### Mar 20 · Apples, Feathers, and Coals
Voltaire, *Letters on the English* — Vol. 34, pp. 113–124
Hook: Voltaire explains how Newton drew world-changing discoveries from everyday things like falling apples.
Note: Sir Isaac Newton died March 20, 1727

### Mar 21 · 1,000 Years of History on the Surface of a Shield
Virgil, *Aeneid* — Vol. 13, pp. 280–292
Hook: Venus coaxes her husband Vulcan into forging a shield for Aeneas engraved with ten centuries of Rome's future.

### Mar 22 · From Puppet Show to Majestic Drama
Goethe, *Faust* — Vol. 19, pp. 23–36
Hook: Goethe turns an old puppet-show legend, the scholar who trades his soul for magic powers, into towering drama.
Note: Goethe died March 22, 1832

### Mar 23 · First of a Thousand Harem Stories
*The Thousand and One Nights* — Vol. 16, pp. 15–24
Hook: Shahrazad's first night: she starts a gripping tale and stops at the best part, buying herself another day.

### Mar 24 · A Queen Pleads
Morris, *Defense of Guenevere* — Vol. 42, pp. 1183–1193
Hook: Accused, with some justice, and treated harshly, Queen Guenevere defends herself and Launcelot before the court.
Note: William Morris born March 24, 1834

### Mar 25 · How Conscience Makes Cowards of Us All
Shakespeare, *Hamlet* — Vol. 46, pp. 144–158
Hook: Hamlet weighs bearing life's troubles against ending them and facing whatever comes after death.
Note: Shakespeare makes his will, March 25, 1616

### Mar 26 · "2,500 Years Ago Aesop Said . . ."
*Aesop's Fables* — Vol. 17, pp. 21–30
Hook: Aesop's little fables, still fresh and still shrewd after twenty-five centuries.
Note: Caxton prints Aesop's Fables, March 26, 1484

### Mar 27 · When Is a Lie Not a Lie?
Stevenson, *Truth of Intercourse* — Vol. 28, pp. 277–284
Hook: Stevenson untangles a puzzle: can a lie carry the truth, and can a true statement still deceive?

### Mar 28 · Pins and Other Points
Adam Smith, *Wealth of Nations* — Vol. 10, pp. 9–17
Hook: Adam Smith uses the humble pin factory to show how dividing up labor multiplies what workers can make.

### Mar 29 · Hero and Goddess Break Engagement
*Epic and Saga* — Vol. 49, pp. 307–317
Hook: Sigurd pledges himself to Brynhild, then forgets her and weds Gudrun, and a tangled Norse love triangle begins.

### Mar 30 · The Plague of Milan
Manzoni, *I Promessi Sposi* — Vol. 21, pp. 500–512
Hook: Manzoni's novel depicts Milan's great plague, when whole families fell ill and died within a day.
Note: Capuchin monks given charge of the plague hospital in Milan, March 30, 1630

### Mar 31 · The Ghastly Whim of John Donne
Walton, *Life of Dr. Donne* — Vol. 15, pp. 364–369
Hook: Walton describes how John Donne had the portrait for his tomb drawn while he stood dressed in his own shroud.
Note: John Donne died March 31, 1631

## April

### Apr 1 · "Oh! to Be in England Now That April's There"
Browning, *Poems* — Vol. 42, pp. 1068–1074
Hook: Even in sunny Italy, Browning grows homesick for the English countryside when spring comes.

### Apr 2 · A Spoon Dances in the Moonlight
Darwin, *Voyage of the Beagle* — Vol. 29, pp. 462–471
Hook: A costumed spoon on a grave seems to dance in the moonlight, one of Darwin's South Seas oddities.

### Apr 3 · Romance with a Happy Ending
Walton, *Life of George Herbert* — Vol. 15, pp. 392–404
Hook: Walton tells how George Herbert's courtship carried both lovers away almost from their first meeting.
Note: George Herbert born April 3, 1593

### Apr 4 · The Mistakes of a Night
*She Stoops to Conquer* — Vol. 18, pp. 205–215
Hook: A young man takes a private home for an inn and treats his host's daughter as the barmaid.
Note: Oliver Goldsmith born April 4, 1774

### Apr 5 · You and Your Dreams
Hobbes, *Leviathan* — Vol. 34, pp. 313–322
Hook: Hobbes examines where dreams come from and coolly weighs the evidence for ghosts and witches.
Note: Hobbes born April 5, 1588

### Apr 6 · Who Is Bad?
Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* — Vol. 2, pp. 243–253
Hook: Every generation defines badness its own way; the emperor Marcus Aurelius searches for a lasting answer.
Note: Marcus Aurelius born April 6, 121 A.D.

### Apr 7 · Nature Guided His Pen
Wordsworth, *Poems* — Vol. 41, pp. 639–651
Hook: Wordsworth lived so close to nature that his poems read as if the countryside had written them.
Note: Wordsworth born April 7, 1770

### Apr 8 · Beware the Vengeful Hounds!
Aeschylus, *The Libation Bearers* — Vol. 8, pp. 111–121
Hook: Orestes raises his sword against his mother to avenge his father, and she warns him of the hounds to come.

### Apr 9 · A Perfect Land in a Wilderness of Waters
Bacon, *New Atlantis* — Vol. 3, pp. 145–155
Hook: Bacon sketches a model society on an undiscovered island beyond Peru, where science serves human welfare.
Note: Francis Bacon died April 9, 1629

### Apr 10 · Americans—by Will of the King
*First Charter of Virginia* — Vol. 43, pp. 49–58
Hook: King James's 1606 grant that let English adventurers plant a colony in Virginia.
Note: King James grants charter to Virginia, April 10, 1606

### Apr 11 · Danger in Being Young and Fair
Goethe, *Faust* — Vol. 19, pp. 115–131
Hook: Faust dazzles innocent Margaret with jewels, and she accepts them without a thought for what may follow.

### Apr 12 · The Perfect Argument
Berkeley, *Three Dialogues* — Vol. 37, pp. 230–240
Hook: Leslie Stephen judged these dialogues the finest model of how to argue in the English language.

### Apr 13 · Michelangelo His Boon Companion
Cellini, *Autobiography* — Vol. 31, pp. 23–35
Hook: Cellini writes about monarchs and the great artists of his age, Michelangelo included, as everyday friends.

### Apr 14 · A Raid on Spanish Treasure in America
Biggs, *Drake's Great Armada* — Vol. 33, pp. 229–242
Hook: Drake's raiders sail for the rich Spanish towns of the New World, ruthless and hungry for plunder.

### Apr 15 · O Captain! My Captain!
Whitman, *Poems* — Vol. 42, pp. 1412–1420
Hook: Whitman's two great elegies for the president he revered, Abraham Lincoln.
Note: Lincoln died April 15, 1865

### Apr 16 · Inside the Gates of Hell
Dante, *Divine Comedy* — Vol. 20, pp. 32–39
Hook: With Virgil as his shield, Dante reaches Dis, a walled city of the damned ringed by a moat of sufferers.
Note: Dante urges attack on the city of Florence, April 16, 1311

### Apr 17 · Benjamin Franklin—Book Salesman
Franklin, *Autobiography* — Vol. 1, pp. 66–77
Hook: In book-starved 1731 Philadelphia, Franklin goes door to door to rally support for a public library.
Note: Benjamin Franklin died April 17, 1790

### Apr 18 · Ready for Adventures and Conquests
Cervantes, *Don Quixote* — Vol. 14, pp. 17–28
Hook: After too many chivalric romances, a threadbare Spanish gentleman dons his ancestor's armor and rides off seeking adventure.
Note: Cervantes receives the last sacraments April 18, 1616

### Apr 19 · Battle of Concord
Emerson, *Concord Hymn* — Vol. 42, pp. 1245–1246
Hook: Emerson's hymn for the Concord fight, whose opening stanza Dr. Eliot praised for packing everything into twenty-eight words.
Note: Fought April 19, 1775

### Apr 20 · Byron Gave His Life for Freedom
Byron, *Poems* — Vol. 41, pp. 801–815
Hook: Byron, who died aiding the Greeks against the Turks, champions liberty in "The Isles of Greece" and "The Prisoner of Chillon."
Note: At Missolonghi, Greece, 37 guns honor Byron, April 20, 1824
