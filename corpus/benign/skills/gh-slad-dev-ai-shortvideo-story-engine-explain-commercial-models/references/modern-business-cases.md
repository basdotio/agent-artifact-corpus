## Modern China

### Alibaba: marketplace plus merchant services

- Connect merchants and consumers; monetize traffic, transaction-related services, logistics coordination, cloud, and other services rather than relying only on owned inventory.
- **Control point**: merchant demand, consumer traffic, data, and commerce infrastructure.
- **Watch**: platform regulation, merchant ROI, competition, subsidy, and ecosystem complexity.
- **Story angle**: the marketplace can earn without owning every product because it owns the place where demand is discovered.

### Tencent: freemium content and ecosystem

- Offer communication and entertainment products at low or zero entry price; monetize games, advertising, payments, cloud, subscriptions, and ecosystem services.
- **Control point**: user attention, identity/social graph, distribution, and developer/content ecosystem.
- **Watch**: regulation, content cycles, platform responsibility, and user trust.
- **Story angle**: the free product may be the distribution system for several paid businesses.

### JD.com: direct retail plus logistics infrastructure

- Combine owned retail inventory with marketplace services and an integrated logistics network.
- **Control point**: fulfillment reliability, supplier relationships, consumer trust, and delivery density.
- **Watch**: inventory, capital intensity, price competition, and thin retail margins.
- **Story angle**: a cost center becomes a product when the logistics system is offered to others.

### Meituan: local-services marketplace and delivery network

- Aggregate restaurants, stores, services, riders, and consumers; monetize transactions, delivery, advertising/merchant tools, and adjacent services.
- **Control point**: local demand, dispatch density, merchant access, and frequent-use consumer entry points.
- **Watch**: labor conditions, subsidy, regulation, merchant economics, and delivery cost.
- **Story angle**: density matters—one more order can improve a route only if geography and timing align.

### Pinduoduo: social demand aggregation

- Use group interaction, discovery shopping, value positioning, and merchant services to aggregate fragmented demand.
- **Control point**: low-cost engagement, demand pooling, merchant competition, and recommendation.
- **Watch**: product quality, merchant returns, subsidy, trust, and regulatory scrutiny.
- **Story angle**: instead of waiting for users to search, manufacture a buying group and let aggregated demand negotiate supply.

### Xiaomi: hardware entry plus ecosystem and services

- Use competitively priced hardware to build an installed base, then monetize services, accessories, software, channels, and an ecosystem of connected products.
- **Control point**: brand/community, device base, channel, software layer, and supply-chain coordination.
- **Watch**: hardware cycles, low margins, ecosystem quality, and competition.
- **Story angle**: the first device may acquire a long-lived customer relationship rather than maximize one-sale margin.

### BYD: vertical integration and learning scale

- Coordinate batteries, components, vehicles, manufacturing, and R&D to improve cost, supply security, and iteration speed.
- **Control point**: critical components, manufacturing learning, scale, and integrated engineering.
- **Watch**: capital expenditure, technology shifts, utilization, price competition, and geopolitics.
- **Story angle**: owning more of the chain can reduce dependency but also concentrates capital risk.

### Haier: user-oriented organization and ecosystem services

- Move beyond selling appliances toward connected products, service ecosystems, and smaller accountable operating units.
- **Control point**: brand, channels, manufacturing, service network, and organizational responsiveness.
- **Watch**: management complexity, accountability quality, and ecosystem claims.
- **Story angle**: changing who makes decisions can be a business-model innovation, not merely an HR reform.

### Pop Mart: IP, collectability, and repeat purchase

- Develop or license characters, sell collectible products, use series design and scarcity, operate retail/vending channels, and extend IP into collaborations.
- **Control point**: character attachment, product design, channel, community, and release cadence.
- **Watch**: hit dependence, inventory, youth marketing, artificial scarcity, and intermittent-reward concerns.
- **Story angle**: the material object is cheap relative to the identity, surprise, and completion desire being sold.

### Luckin Coffee: digital ordering and standardized store network

- Use app-based ordering, promotions, standardized products, compact formats, supply-chain control, and dense stores.
- **Control point**: digital customer data, menu iteration, procurement, and store density.
- **Watch**: discount dependence, store cannibalization, accounting trust, and quality consistency.
- **Story angle**: a beverage company can behave like a software-enabled retail network, but data does not excuse governance failures.

### Trip.com Group: travel aggregation

- Aggregate travel inventory and consumer demand; monetize commissions, service fees, advertising, packages, and support.
- **Control point**: search, availability, trust, customer service, and supplier connectivity.
- **Watch**: travel cycles, supplier bargaining, customer acquisition, refunds, and platform competition.
- **Story angle**: the intermediary earns by reducing comparison and coordination cost across fragmented inventory.

## Modern global/Western cases

### Costco: membership funds low-margin retail loyalty

- Charge annual membership while maintaining a limited assortment and value-focused retail operation.
- **Control point**: member trust, renewal, purchasing scale, and efficient inventory.
- **Watch**: renewal, wage/operating cost, supplier terms, and expansion discipline.
- **Story angle**: the product margin attracts and retains members; the membership changes the profit structure.

### McDonald's: franchise system plus real estate and supply standards

- Franchise operating rights, collect rent and royalties, standardize brand/process/supply, and operate some restaurants directly.
- **Control point**: brand, locations, operating system, franchisee network, and purchasing scale.
- **Watch**: franchisee economics, food inflation, brand consistency, and property commitments.
- **Story angle**: the visible hamburger business is also a system, royalty, and location business.

### Amazon: flywheel of selection, traffic, sellers, infrastructure, and membership

- Combine direct retail, third-party marketplace fees, fulfillment, membership, advertising, and cloud infrastructure.
- **Control point**: consumer demand, logistics, seller access, computing infrastructure, and accumulated scale.
- **Watch**: capital intensity, regulation, labor, marketplace conflicts, and cross-subsidy claims.
- **Story angle**: infrastructure built for an internal bottleneck can become a higher-margin external service.

### Apple: integrated device ecosystem and services

- Sell premium devices while services, accessories, software distribution, and ecosystem integration increase retention and lifetime value.
- **Control point**: brand, hardware/software integration, installed base, developer access, and switching cost.
- **Watch**: product concentration, platform regulation, supply chain, and upgrade cycles.
- **Story angle**: the first hardware sale creates a governed environment for later transactions.

### Alphabet/Google: free utility funded by intent advertising

- Offer search and consumer tools broadly, then sell advertisers access to measurable intent and audiences; also monetize cloud and subscriptions.
- **Control point**: attention, intent data, distribution defaults, auction systems, and infrastructure.
- **Watch**: privacy, antitrust, AI changes, traffic-acquisition cost, and advertiser ROI.
- **Story angle**: when the user does not pay, identify the paying customer and what access is being purchased.

### Microsoft: licenses to subscription and cloud consumption

- Monetize software through licenses, recurring subscriptions, cloud usage, enterprise contracts, developer tools, gaming, and ecosystem services.
- **Control point**: enterprise workflow integration, compatibility, installed base, developer ecosystem, and cloud scale.
- **Watch**: security, regulation, bundling, migration cost, and product complexity.
- **Story angle**: convert a periodic upgrade sale into an ongoing workflow relationship.

### Salesforce: SaaS subscription and ecosystem

- Deliver enterprise software through recurring subscriptions, add modules, services, integrations, and partner applications.
- **Control point**: customer data/workflow, switching cost, platform ecosystem, and sales organization.
- **Watch**: acquisition cost, implementation complexity, seat growth, and churn.
- **Story angle**: predictable revenue is purchased with continuous product and customer-success obligations.

### Netflix: subscription plus content portfolio

- Charge recurring access, invest in licensed/original content, personalize discovery, and spread content cost across a subscriber base.
- **Control point**: subscriber relationship, recommendation, brand, and content portfolio.
- **Watch**: churn, content cost, hit uncertainty, regional pricing, and competition.
- **Story angle**: one hit attracts attention, but a portfolio and retention system make subscription economics work.

### Visa: network toll without ordinary consumer lending

- Operate payment-network infrastructure and earn service/data-processing/international and related fees as authorized institutions issue cards and extend most credit.
- **Control point**: acceptance network, standards, reliability, security, and bank relationships.
- **Watch**: regulation, fee pressure, fraud, alternative rails, and concentration.
- **Story angle**: own the trusted rail and collect a tiny amount across enormous transaction volume.

### Arm: architecture licensing and royalties

- License processor designs/architecture and collect royalties when partners ship chips.
- **Control point**: IP, ecosystem compatibility, developer adoption, and low-power expertise.
- **Watch**: customer concentration, alternative architectures, geopolitics, and licensing disputes.
- **Story angle**: participate in millions of products without manufacturing each chip.

### Bloomberg: must-have information terminal

- Sell high-priced subscriptions combining real-time data, analytics, communication, and workflow.
- **Control point**: proprietary/organized data, speed, professional network, and workflow switching cost.
- **Watch**: data rights, niche concentration, and alternative platforms.
- **Story angle**: information becomes durable subscription revenue when it is embedded in the minute-by-minute job.

### IKEA: flat-pack and customer co-production

- Design for flat packaging, self-service warehouses, standardized manufacturing, and customer assembly to lower logistics and labor cost.
- **Control point**: design-to-cost, store experience, procurement scale, and brand.
- **Watch**: quality, sourcing, large-store economics, and customer inconvenience.
- **Story angle**: transfer selected work to customers while lowering price enough that they accept the trade.

### Inditex/Zara: fast-response fashion supply chain

- Use rapid feedback, smaller batches, coordinated design/production/distribution, and frequent store refreshes.
- **Control point**: information speed and supply-chain responsiveness rather than only low manufacturing cost.
- **Watch**: sustainability, labor, inventory, fashion volatility, and imitation.
- **Story angle**: reduce forecast risk by buying speed and smaller commitments.

### Airbnb: asset-light accommodation marketplace

- Match hosts and guests, provide discovery, payment, reputation, and support, then charge service fees.
- **Control point**: two-sided liquidity, reviews, brand, trust systems, and demand aggregation.
- **Watch**: local regulation, safety, quality variance, disintermediation, and travel cycles.
- **Story angle**: coordinate underused assets without owning all of them, while inheriting trust and regulation problems.

### Uber: on-demand mobility marketplace

- Match riders and drivers, price and dispatch dynamically, process payments, and expand into adjacent delivery/logistics services.
- **Control point**: local liquidity, dispatch data, consumer habit, and payment interface.
- **Watch**: driver economics, regulation, insurance, subsidy, and local density.
- **Story angle**: a marketplace becomes useful only when supply arrives fast enough at the exact local moment.

### Gillette-style razor-and-blade

- Lower the barrier to the durable device and earn recurring margin from compatible consumables.
- **Control point**: installed base, compatibility, habit, and distribution.
- **Watch**: customer resentment, generic alternatives, and regulation of lock-in.
- **Story angle**: the first sale creates the right to compete for many later purchases.

### Dell direct model and working capital

- Sell configured computers directly, use customer orders to inform production, reduce channel inventory, and historically benefit from favorable cash timing.
- **Control point**: direct demand data, configuration, supplier coordination, and inventory discipline.
- **Watch**: component cycles, supplier dependency, demand volatility, and service.
- **Story angle**: receive the order before committing all inventory, turning information speed into cash advantage.

## Comparison prompts

- Alibaba marketplace versus JD direct retail: who owns inventory and fulfillment risk?
- Costco membership versus Amazon Prime: what does the recurring fee fund and reinforce?
- McDonald's franchise versus company-owned chains: who supplies capital and bears store-level risk?
- Apple ecosystem versus Arm licensing: integrate the stack or license a layer?
- Meituan versus Uber: how does local density change dispatch economics?
- Pop Mart versus Netflix: hit-driven IP products versus subscription content portfolios.
- Jingdezhen cluster versus BYD integration: coordinate specialized outsiders or own critical stages?
- Piaohao remittance versus Visa: how do trusted claims replace physical movement of money?
