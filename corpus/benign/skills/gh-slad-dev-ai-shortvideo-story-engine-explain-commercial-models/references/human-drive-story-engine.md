## Design rule

Use emotion to open attention, a human story to maintain it, and a commercial mechanism to reward the viewer. Do not merely attach a financial lecture after an unrelated dramatic story.

The protagonist's desire must cause the transaction, habit, or business decision being explained.

## Drive-to-business map

| Human drive | Useful business topics | Emotional risk | Required correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status/dignity | luxury, branding, career signaling, entrepreneurship | glamorizing consumption | show cash flow and opportunity cost |
| Romance/validation | beauty, gifts, dating content, weddings, courses | gender hostility | map anxiety creation and mutual agency |
| Greed/shortcut | scams, arbitrage, leverage, investing | promising easy wealth | show base rate, maximum loss, and legality |
| Better life/security | housing, insurance, education, side hustles | exploiting fear | offer realistic thresholds and alternatives |
| Belonging/brotherhood | games, communities, clubs, referrals | tribal hostility | give a constructive share reason |
| Intermittent reward | games, blind boxes, trading, live streams | normalizing addiction | explain reward loop and exit friction |
| Justice/exposure | fraud, contracts, audits, workplace incentives | procedural wrongdoing | focus on detection and prevention |
| Approval/signaling | productivity, office culture, creator tools | mocking the weak | distinguish signals from measurable value |

## Share-recipient map

Before writing, finish this sentence:

> A viewer will send this to `[specific person]` because it helps them say `[message]`.

Examples:

- brother/game teammate: “we enjoy it, but do not let the virtual scoreboard eat real life”;
- partner: “let us judge care by repeated behavior, not staged tests”;
- colleague: “visible busyness is not customer value”;
- aspiring entrepreneur: “calculate cash timing before copying the revenue screenshot”;
- parent/sibling: “this financial promise is selling our security back to us.”

If no recipient is obvious, design for saving instead: provide a checklist, formula, comparison table, or red-flag list.

## Story construction

### A. Desire-cost-commercial triangle

Write three sentences before scripting:

1. The protagonist wants `[emotionally concrete outcome]`.
2. The protagonist pays `[money/time/attention/trust/risk]` to pursue it.
3. `[business actor]` earns because it controls `[distribution, uncertainty, status signal, financing, data, or habit]`.

Reject the topic if the three sentences do not causally connect.

### B. Two scoreboards

Track one visible and one hidden scoreboard throughout the episode.

Examples:

- followers rising / cash falling;
- game inventory rising / sleep and relationship falling;
- revenue rising / receivables and debt rising;
- luxury symbols rising / free cash flow falling;
- hours worked rising / customer outcomes unchanged;
- discounts rising / gross margin falling.

Make the final reversal occur when the visible scoreboard peaks and the hidden scoreboard collapses.

### C. Reward-to-dependence escalation

1. First reward arrives cheaply.
2. The protagonist attributes it to skill, love, status, or secret knowledge.
3. Repetition requires more time, money, or risk.
4. Small warning signs are rationalized.
5. A deadline forces the conflict into the open.
6. The system collects its revenue while the protagonist absorbs the downside.

### D. Status-reversal with real business causality

Do not jump from hardship montage to wealth. Show at least three mechanisms:

- first validated customer problem;
- offer and price;
- acquisition channel;
- delivery system;
- gross margin or cash cycle;
- hiring or automation threshold;
- major failure and correction.

### E. Commercial thriller

Use procedural suspense around legitimate verification:

- due diligence;
- reconciling invoices to bank receipts;
- mystery shopping;
- tracing product origin;
- testing unit economics;
- discovering a contract clause;
- finding a policy dependency.

Withhold the overlooked variable, not instructions for wrongdoing.

## Visual direction

Give each story one dominant visual grammar:

- **Self-deception**: cold classrooms/offices, symmetrical compositions, colorful props that look productive, harsh reality frame at the test.
- **Relationship/validation**: warm domestic reality versus bright, over-polished phone content; show the screen as an intruder.
- **Aspiration/brotherhood**: low-angle status objects, wide hometown or workplace frames, restrained black-gold accent, warm light for the loyal ally.
- **Addiction/circle identity**: dark room, HUD overlays, green/red reward colors, rising virtual inventory beside degrading physical environment.
- **Thriller/audit**: clocks, documents, fingerprints, doors, corridors, desaturated blue-green light, quiet close-ups.

Keep character design consistent within an episode. Use symbolic interfaces with original labels rather than copying a game's or creator's protected UI.

## Editing baseline

- 4-6 minute story: target 1,400-2,000 Chinese narration characters.
- Narration: start around 5.0-5.8 Chinese characters per second.
- Major visual refresh: start around every 6-8 seconds.
- Retention reset: every 20-40 seconds.
- Use a hard cut for the first reward, irreversible decision, deadline collision, and final reveal.
- Use slower dissolves for memory, ordinary life, or aftermath.
- Reduce music or insert a brief silence before the sentence that changes the story's meaning.
- Highlight only causal numbers and phrases. Do not turn every sentence into animated typography.

## Safety and trust

- Use fictionalized scenarios or verified cases; label them clearly.
- Do not intensify sexual, romantic, class, or gender resentment to manufacture comments.
- Do not portray luxury as proof of virtue or poverty as proof of failure.
- Do not include operational wrongdoing or evasion.
- Preserve the audience's dignity: explain why a smart ordinary person could be influenced.
- Give one realistic prevention or recovery behavior at the end.
