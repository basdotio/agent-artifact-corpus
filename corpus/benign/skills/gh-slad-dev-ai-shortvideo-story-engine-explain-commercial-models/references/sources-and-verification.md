# Sources and verification

## Source hierarchy

For publishable scripts, prefer:

1. law, regulator, court, government archive, museum collection, or original historical document;
2. audited filing, annual report, prospectus, official investor presentation, or company policy;
3. academic book, journal, university, or reputable institutional history;
4. high-quality journalism with named evidence;
5. encyclopedia or Wikipedia for discovery only;
6. creator video, forum, or unsourced post only as a claim to verify.

For every material claim save:

- exact claim;
- source title and URL;
- publication/reporting period;
- quoted or extracted evidence;
- whether it is fact, interpretation, estimate, or disputed claim;
- what changed since publication.

## Verification rules

- Never rely on a video narrator's attributed quotation without locating the original context.
- Recalculate all interest, compounding, margin, take-rate, and cash-cycle examples.
- Separate company revenue mix from profit mix and cash flow.
- Do not infer a company's current model from an old famous story; read its latest filing.
- For private companies, state the evidence limitation.
- For “first ever,” “largest,” “only,” and “guaranteed” claims, require unusually strong evidence or remove the superlative.
- For historical stories, distinguish documented institutions from fictional composite characters.
- For regulated activities, check the relevant jurisdiction and current law before describing a method as executable.

## Script citation practice

Short videos need not display long footnotes during narration, but should provide:

- a compact source card at the end;
- source titles or links in the caption/pinned comment when practical;
- explicit wording such as “according to the company's annual report” or “this is a simplified fictional example”;
- correction notes when a later source changes the conclusion.
